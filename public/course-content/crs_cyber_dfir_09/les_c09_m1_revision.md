## Revision

### Summary

Digital forensics applies scientific method to digital evidence. Its principles are integrity, documentation, repeatability, minimal intrusion, competence, objectivity and chain of custody. NIST SP 800-86 (2006) gives four phases: collection, examination, analysis and reporting. Evidence lives on endpoints, servers, networks, cloud, mobile devices and security tools, and collection follows the RFC 3227 order of volatility. In India, BSA Section 63 (from 1 July 2024) governs electronic records with a certificate. Under the predecessor Section 65B, Khotkar (2020) held the certificate to be a condition precedent, upholding Anvar P.V. (2014). Ethics demand authority, proportionality, objectivity, confidentiality and honesty about limits.

### Important concepts

questions drive scope; integrity and documentation; legal admissibility.

### Key technical takeaways

order of volatility; collection plans with method, authority and storage.

### Common mistakes

collecting without authority; undocumented live actions; equating account activity with a person.
