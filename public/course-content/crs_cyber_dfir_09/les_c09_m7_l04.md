Short-term containment stops spread and damage: isolate hosts, block attacker infrastructure, disable or reset accounts and revoke sessions. Long-term containment keeps the business running safely while eradication is prepared, for example through segmentation, temporary firewall rules, heightened monitoring, or moving a service behind additional authentication. Timing is the key judgment:

- Contain immediately when damage is active (ransomware encrypting, data leaving, safety at risk).
- Consider a short delay to observe when the attacker is quiet and scoping is incomplete. Containing one host can alert an attacker who still holds others, prompting them to change tools or cause damage. The decision belongs to the incident lead, with legal input, and the risk must be recorded.
- Coordinate containment across all known footholds at once where possible ("pull the plug" day).

Evidence first, where safe: acquire memory and key artefacts before actions that destroy them (Module 4). Choose containment that keeps evidence, such as EDR network isolation rather than power-off.
