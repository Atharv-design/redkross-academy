Digital forensics applies scientific methods to identify, preserve, collect, examine, analyse and present digital evidence. Sub-disciplines include computer (disk) forensics, memory forensics, network forensics, mobile forensics, cloud forensics and forensic analysis of logs and applications. Forensics differs from incident response in emphasis. IR prioritises stopping harm quickly; forensics prioritises accuracy and defensibility. In practice they run together (DFIR): IR decisions such as isolating a host should be taken with evidence in mind (Course 08 Module 7), and forensic findings drive scoping and eradication. Questions forensics can answer include who, what, when, where and how. It often cannot answer why, and it frequently cannot prove who was physically at the keyboard. "Account divya did X" is not the same as "Divya did X".
