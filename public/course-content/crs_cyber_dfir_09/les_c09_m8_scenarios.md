Work through each scenario on your own first, then compare your reasoning with the model answer.

## Scenario 1

The client asks you to remove a limitation from your report "because it confuses the board".

**Task:** Respond.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Decline to remove it. Offer to explain it more simply in the executive summary, or to move the detail to an appendix while keeping a clear statement in the body. Explain that omitting material limitations would make the report misleading and indefensible.
>
> **Lesson:** presentation can change, substance cannot.

## Scenario 2

After release, you discover a tool version you used had a parsing bug affecting one finding.

**Task:** Act.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Verify the bug's effect by re-analysing with a fixed version or another tool. Issue a corrected or supplementary report with a version note, explaining the change and its impact. Inform the client and legal counsel promptly, and record the tool issue in your validation log.
>
> **Lesson:** candour after release, as in the Casey Anthony tool discrepancy.

## Scenario 3

In cross-examination you are asked whether the defendant typed a command.

**Task:** Answer.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> **Answer within the evidence:** "The artefacts show the command was executed in a session of account X at time T. They do not show who was at the keyboard." Mention any corroborating or conflicting evidence you examined. Do not speculate.
>
> **Lesson:** an account is not a person.
