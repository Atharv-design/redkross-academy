## Scenario 1

A manager asks you to "quietly check" an employee's personal Gmail from the company laptop.

**Task:** Respond.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Decline. A personal account is outside company authority without consent or lawful process, and accessing it may be an offence under the IT Act and a privacy breach. Offer lawful alternatives: review company systems within policy and scope, and involve legal and HR. Document the request.
>
> **Lesson:** authority defines what you may examine.

## Scenario 2

Legal asks whether your exported logs can be used in court.

**Task:** Explain what you can provide.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Describe the source system, the export method and query, hashes, chain-of-custody records, tool versions and clock information, and offer to support a Section 63 certificate. Note limitations, such as the SIEM's own integrity. The decision on admissibility and certification belongs to legal counsel and the court.
>
> **Lesson:** provide the facts that certification relies on.

## Scenario 3

You find evidence that contradicts the incident lead's theory.

**Task:** Act.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Verify the finding (method, second tool or source). Document it, present it promptly to the incident lead with its confidence, and include it in the report even if unwelcome. If pressured to omit it, escalate.
>
> **Lesson:** facts outrank theories.
