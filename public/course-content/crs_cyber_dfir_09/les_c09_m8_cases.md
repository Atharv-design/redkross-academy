## Bates v Post Office (Horizon Issues), 2019 ------------------------------------------------------------------ Context: In group litigation brought by sub-postmasters against Post Office Limited, the High Court (Fraser J) handed down Judgment No. 6, "Horizon Issues", on 16 December 2019 ([2019] EWHC 3408 (QB)). The judgment examined the Horizon accounting system, used in post office branches since about 2000. It concluded that "Legacy Horizon was not remotely robust", citing "the number, extent and type of impact of the numerous bugs, errors and defects" the court had found, and that robustness improved over time. The judgment also records that Fujitsu witnesses eventually accepted the ability to obtain remote access to branch accounts without sub-postmaster knowledge or permission. This was access "of a far wider nature than admitted to by the Post Office or Fujitsu publicly" [C09-M08-R1]. Lessons for examiners: - Computer output is evidence about a system, and the system's reliability must itself be examined rather than assumed. - Known defects, remote-access capabilities and audit-data limits are material limitations and must be disclosed. - Expert evidence was central to establishing the facts. Independence, candour and willingness to revise statements matter. - The consequences of unexamined computer evidence fall on real people. Verification note: quotations are from the judgment text retrieved on 2026-11-07. The wider history (prosecutions, appeals and the public inquiry) is beyond this case study. ## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The draft that said "proves" ------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. A draft report on a Tamarind laptop said the USB artefacts "prove that Priya copied the design files". Peer review pointed out that the session was unlocked while Priya was in a recorded meeting (calendar and badge data supplied by HR), and that another employee had used the laptop that afternoon. The final report said the artefacts showed copying to the device during account priya's session, noted the conflicting presence evidence, and stated that the person responsible could not be determined from the digital evidence alone. Lesson: peer review and calibrated language prevent wrongful conclusions.
