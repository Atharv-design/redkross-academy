## Revision

### Summary

Reports serve executives, technical teams and legal processes, with consistent facts throughout. A defensible structure moves from questions and authority through evidence (IDs, hashes), methods, timeline, findings, limitations and conclusions, to appendices. In the tested appendix, E1's hash matched its capture-time hash, proving integrity at report time. Timeline entries carry sources and UTC times. Findings separate observation from interpretation and use a defined certainty scale, never equating accounts with people. Limitations and alternative explanations are disclosed. Peer review, reproducibility and version control precede release. The Horizon Issues judgment, which found Legacy Horizon "not remotely robust" and documented remote access, shows why system reliability and limitations must be examined and reported.

### Important concepts

traceability; calibrated certainty; candour.

### Key technical takeaways

evidence appendix with hashes; commands in appendices; tool versions.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|

### Common mistakes

"proves" without basis; missing limitations; new material in conclusions.
