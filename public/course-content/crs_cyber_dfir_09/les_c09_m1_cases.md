## Arjun Panditrao Khotkar (2020) - the certificate as a condition precedent ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: Conflicting Supreme Court decisions on whether a Section 65B(4) certificate was mandatory for electronic records (Anvar P.V., 2014, versus Shafhi Mohammad, 2018) were referred to a three-judge bench. On 14 July 2020 the Court held that the certificate is a condition precedent to the admissibility of evidence by way of electronic record. It upheld Anvar P.V. and found the "clarification" in Shafhi Mohammad incorrect (para 59) [C09-M01-R3]. Forensic lessons: - Technical correctness is not enough. Evidence must also meet procedural requirements to be used. - Examiners should preserve the information a certificate relies on: device identity, how the record was produced, and hashes. - The BSA (Section 63) now governs, but the reasoning remains influential. Confirm current practice with legal counsel.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The over-collection ------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. In a misconduct inquiry about one email, an internal investigator imaged the employee's entire personal phone "to be safe", without consent or legal process, and browsed family photos. The employee's lawyer challenged the collection. The inquiry was compromised, and the company faced a privacy complaint. Lessons: define questions and scope first; obtain authority; collect proportionately; and keep personal devices out of scope unless there is consent or lawful process.
