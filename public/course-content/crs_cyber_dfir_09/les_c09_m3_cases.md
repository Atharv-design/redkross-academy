## Casey Anthony trial, 2011 - one visit or 84? -------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: In the 2011 Florida murder trial of Casey Anthony, prosecutors presented browser-history evidence suggesting that a family computer had visited a page about chloroform 84 times. After the trial, contemporaneous news reports quoted the designer of the CacheBack software, John Bradley, as saying that his tool's analysis was wrong: the site had been visited once, consistent with a second tool, NetAnalysis. He reported that he had discovered the discrepancy during the trial and informed prosecutors [C09-M03-R3]. Prosecutors disputed aspects of how the issue was communicated. Lessons: - Forensic tools parse complex artefacts, such as browser history databases, and can be wrong. - Where a finding matters, verify it with a second tool or manual analysis of the underlying structure. - Disclose discrepancies promptly. Duties of candour apply to experts. - Report counts and timestamps with their source and method, not just a tool's summary. Verification note: details are from multiple contemporaneous news reports found by web search. Court transcripts were not reviewed (SME review recommended).



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The copied folder ----------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. A departing Tamarind employee's USB drive (FAT32) held a folder of design files. Their "created" times were all the afternoon before resignation, but their "modified" times went back months. The examiner explained that the pattern was consistent with copying, since copies receive new creation times. Combined with USB device history from the laptop's registry (Module 5), it supported a finding of copying to that drive on that afternoon. The examiner also noted that FAT stores local time and confirmed the laptop's time zone.
