## The xz Utils backdoor, 2024 - half a second of evidence ---------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: On 29 March 2024, Andres Freund posted to the oss-security mailing list under the subject "backdoor in upstream xz/liblzma leading to ssh server compromise". He had noticed odd symptoms on Debian sid, including SSH logins taking a lot of CPU and valgrind errors, and measured failed SSH logins taking about 0.8 seconds instead of about 0.3. Investigating, he found injected code in the xz 5.6.0 and 5.6.1 release tarballs [C09-M06-R1]. CISA issued an alert the same day (CVE-2024-3094), advising downgrade to an uncompromised version such as 5.4.6 [C09-M06-R2]. Lessons: - Small, measurable anomalies on a Linux host (CPU use, latency) were the first evidence of a sophisticated supply-chain backdoor. - Careful measurement and documentation turned a hunch into a reproducible finding that others could verify. - Package-level evidence (versions, tarball contents) is as important as logs in Linux investigations.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The missing hour ---------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. An examiner at Tamarind built a timeline from a server's auth.log (local time IST, no zone marked) and firewall logs (UTC). The download appeared five and a half hours before the attacker's login, and the draft report suggested a second, unknown intruder. A reviewer checked timedatectl output collected at acquisition, found the server ran in Asia/Kolkata, and corrected the auth.log times. The "second intruder" disappeared. Lesson: normalise time zones before interpreting order.
