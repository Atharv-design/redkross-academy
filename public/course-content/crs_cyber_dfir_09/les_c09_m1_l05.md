This lesson summarises points relevant to examiners. It is not legal advice. Course 01 Module 7 contains the wider framework and SME-review flags.

- Admissibility. The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA), in force from 1 July 2024, replaced the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Section 63 of the BSA governs the admissibility of electronic records, succeeding Section 65B, and requires a certificate in the form in its Schedule. Commentators note that the form includes details such as hash values of the record (SME review recommended).
- Case law under Section 65B, likely to inform interpretation of Section 63. In Anvar P.V. v. P.K. Basheer ((2014) 10 SCC 473), a three-judge bench of the Supreme Court held that electronic records offered as secondary evidence require the Section 65B certificate. In Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal (judgment of 14 July 2020, R.F. Nariman J for a three-judge bench), the Court reiterated that "the certificate required under Section 65B(4) is a condition precedent to the admissibility of evidence by way of electronic record, as correctly held in Anvar P.V. ... and incorrectly 'clarified' in Shafhi Mohammed" (para 59) [R3]. Commentary on the judgment notes that a certificate is unnecessary when the original device itself is produced, and that a court can be asked to order a certificate from the person in control of the device. Read the judgment for the full reasoning.
- Experts. Section 79A of the Information Technology Act, 2000 allows the Central Government to notify Examiners of Electronic Evidence (SME review for the current list and procedure).
- Procedure. The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) governs criminal procedure, including search and seizure. Corporate examiners are not police and have no powers of search over other people's property.
- Privacy. Personal data in evidence is subject to the DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025 as they commence (Course 01 Module 7). Collect only what is needed, secure it, and restrict access.
- Reporting duties. CERT-In Directions (6-hour reporting; 180-day logs) may apply in parallel (Course 08 Module 7).

**Practical implications for examiners:** keep originals or verified images; record hashes and the systems used; be ready to support a Section 63 certificate; document authority for every collection.
