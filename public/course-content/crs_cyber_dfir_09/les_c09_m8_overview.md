## About this module

A forensic investigation is only as good as its report. The report is what managers act on, what lawyers rely on, what opposing experts attack, and what a court may read years later. This final module teaches how to present an evidence timeline, state findings with the right degree of certainty, disclose limitations and alternative explanations, and structure a report that another examiner could check. It closes with the course capstone.

## Why it matters

Overstated or unexplained computer evidence can ruin lives. In the UK Post Office Horizon litigation, the High Court found in 2019 that the legacy Horizon system "was not remotely robust", after years in which its figures had been relied on against sub-postmasters (case study). Defensible reporting is a safeguard for the people affected by findings, not just a writing skill.

## Where it is used

Internal investigation reports, IR reports for boards and regulators, expert reports in civil and criminal proceedings, insurer and audit reports.

## Learning objectives

1. Structure a forensic report for its audience and purpose.
2. Present an evidence timeline with sources for every entry.
3. Express findings using calibrated language of certainty.
4. Disclose methods, tools, limitations and alternative explanations.
5. Apply peer review and quality checks before release.
6. Complete the capstone investigation and report.
