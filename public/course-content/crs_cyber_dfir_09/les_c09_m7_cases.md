## Maersk and NotPetya, 2017 - recovery at scale --------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: In June 2017 the NotPetya malware spread worldwide from a compromised Ukrainian accounting software update and destroyed systems at many organisations. Speaking at the World Economic Forum in Davos in January 2018, A.P. Moller-Maersk's chairman Jim Hagemann Snabe said the company had to reinstall its infrastructure: 4,000 servers, 45,000 PCs and 2,500 applications, in what he called a heroic effort over about ten days, during which much work was done manually [C09-M07-R2]. The company estimated costs of USD 250-300 million. Lessons: - For destructive attacks, recovery means rebuilding at scale, which is feasible only with prepared images, procedures and people. - Manual business continuity procedures kept operations going. - An organisation can be collateral damage from an attack aimed at others. Supply-chain and third-party software risk belongs in preparation. Verification note: the figures come from multiple reports of the Davos remarks (web-search cross-check). The original panel recording was not reviewed.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The restored backdoor --------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. After ransomware, a company restored its file servers from the most recent backup and went back to business. Two weeks later the attacker returned. The backup had been taken after the attacker installed a scheduled task, and a compromised service account had never been reset. Lessons: restore from backups that pre-date the compromise (or remove persistence from them); reset the credentials found in scoping; and monitor restored systems closely.
