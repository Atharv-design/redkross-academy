## About this module

Consumers love names. "Who is behind it?" is usually the first question after an incident, and the hardest to answer honestly. This module separates what defenders can establish with evidence - activity clusters, campaigns, behaviour patterns - from what is usually beyond their reach: the identity and sponsor of an adversary. You will structure MONSOON LANTERN with the Diamond Model, build its timeline, and test the popular shortcut of matching observed techniques against ATT&CK group profiles. The lab result is the lesson: six groups match the campaign's techniques equally well.

## Why it matters

Wrong attribution misdirects defence, damages credibility and, at national level, can have diplomatic consequences. Adversaries know analysts look for familiar fingerprints and sometimes plant them: in 2018 the Olympic Destroyer wiper carried a code artefact deliberately copied from samples of another group (case study CS1).

## Where it is used

Campaign tracking in CTI teams, incident reports, board briefings, sharing communities, vendor research, and law-enforcement and government attribution (which uses sources unavailable to most organisations).

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish threat actor, intrusion set, campaign and activity cluster, and handle vendor naming differences.
2. Model intrusion events with the Diamond Model and link them into activity threads.
3. Describe adversary behaviour patterns as a tradecraft profile.
4. Cluster events into a campaign with a timeline and competing hypotheses.
5. Explain the levels and limits of attribution, including false flags and technique overlap.
6. Communicate attribution judgments with confidence and alternatives.
