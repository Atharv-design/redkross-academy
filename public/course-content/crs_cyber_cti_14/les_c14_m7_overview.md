## About this module

Indicators expire in weeks; behaviour lasts. MITRE ATT&CK gives defenders a shared vocabulary for adversary behaviour, and mapping intelligence to it turns reports into questions the defence can answer: can we see this technique, do we detect it, have we mitigated it? This module teaches careful mapping from evidence, the structure of ATT&CK 20.1 (including its detection strategies, analytics and data components), gap analysis against what an organisation actually collects, and the derivation of defensive use cases. In the lab, MONSOON LANTERN's nine techniques map to one covered, six partially covered and two blind techniques for Nilgiri.

## Why it matters

A heat map coloured from vendor reports looks like insight but often hides the question that matters: if this behaviour happened in our estate, would we see it? CISA's mapping guidance (version 2.0, January 2023) begins its steps with "Find the behavior" and covers analytical biases and mapping mistakes [R2]. Mapping well is a skill.

## Where it is used

Threat-informed defence programmes, detection engineering backlogs (Course 20), purple-team planning (Course 19), security investment cases, and sharing (ATT&CK identifiers in STIX).

## Learning objectives

1. Describe the structure of ATT&CK 20.1 and handle version changes.
2. Map behaviour in reports to tactics, techniques and sub-techniques with evidence.
3. Use detection strategies, analytics and data components to assess visibility.
4. Perform a gap analysis against an organisation's data inventory.
5. Derive and prioritise defensive use cases from gaps.
6. Communicate mappings with Navigator layers and state their limits.

## Before you start
