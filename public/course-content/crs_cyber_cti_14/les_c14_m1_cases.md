## Log4Shell and requirements under pressure, 2021 Context: CVE-2021-44228 ("Log4Shell"), disclosed in December 2021, allowed remote code execution through JNDI lookups in Apache Log4j versions 2.0-beta9 to 2.14.1. CISA published guidance recommending upgrades (to 2.17.1, 2.12.4 or 2.3.2 depending on the Java version) and issued Emergency Directive 22-02 on 17 December 2021, requiring federal agencies to act [C14-M01-R3]. What happened to intelligence teams: within days, every organisation's standing requirements were overtaken by new, urgent ones. "Where do we run Log4j?" (internal collection: inventories, SBOMs, scans of our own estate), "Is it being exploited against organisations like us?" (external collection: advisories, sharing communities) and "Which vendors' products we use are affected?" (supplier advisories) arrived from different consumers at once, with guidance changing as new versions were released. Lessons: - Requirements must be revisable in hours, with a named owner who decides priorities when everything is urgent. - The most valuable collection was often internal (asset and software inventory), not external threat data. - Dissemination needed versioned products ("this replaces yesterday's advice") because the facts changed quickly.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The feed nobody read Context: Nilgiri Mutual (fictional) bought two commercial feeds and a platform to aggregate them. The CTI analyst produced a weekly 30-page PDF of "top threats" compiled from vendor blogs. What happened: a year later, an internal review found that the SOC ignored the PDF, the CISO had never read beyond page one, and no detection, block or patch decision could be traced to the team's output. Meanwhile the board had asked twice whether insurers were being targeted for policyholder data, and nobody had answered. Response: the team ran consumer interviews, wrote three PIRs with owners (the requirements in data/requirements.yaml), replaced the weekly PDF with a one-page monthly assessment for the CISO, machine-readable indicators for the SOC and a quarterly board paragraph, and started recording feedback and actions taken. Lessons: start from consumers' decisions; match products to levels; measure actions, not volume.
