This lesson is an orientation, not legal advice.

- Information Technology Act, 2000: section 43 covers acts such as accessing a computer system without permission of its owner; section 66 makes such acts an offence when done dishonestly or fraudulently, punishable with imprisonment of up to three years or a fine of up to five lakh rupees or both [R4]. Accessing non-public areas, using leaked credentials, or bypassing access controls is outside OSINT and outside this course.
- Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (No. 22 of 2023, 11 August 2023): section 3(c)(ii) states that the Act does not apply to personal data made or caused to be made publicly available by the Data Principal, or by any other person under a legal obligation to publish it [R2]. This exclusion is narrow: data leaked by a criminal was not made public by the Data Principal, and other laws, contracts and ethics still apply. The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified in November 2025 with phased commencement; check current status with counsel.
- CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 under section 70B(6) of the IT Act: covered entities must report specified cyber incidents to CERT-In within 6 hours of noticing them and keep logs of ICT systems for a rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction [R3]. Intelligence that reveals an incident at your organisation may start that clock.
- Platform terms of service, copyright and contracts (for example commercial feed licences restricting redistribution) also bind collection and sharing.
- Foreign data and foreign laws (for example the EU GDPR for EU residents' data) may apply to multinational investigations.

Write these boundaries into the OSINT policy, train analysts, and route uncertain cases to legal counsel before collecting.
