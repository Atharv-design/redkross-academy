## The Berkeley Protocol, 2020 Context: on 1 December 2020 the Human Rights Center at UC Berkeley and the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights launched the Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations, "a practical guide on the effective use of digital open source information in investigating violations of international criminal, human rights and humanitarian law" (the published edition carries the date 2022) [C14-M02-R1]. Why it matters: open-source material had become central to investigations, but practices varied widely, which made findings hard to defend. The Protocol sets out preparation (risk and landscape assessments, investigation plans, data policies), an investigation process (online inquiries, preliminary assessment, collection, preservation, verification, investigative analysis) and principles including minimising harm, protecting privacy, data minimisation and security by design. Lessons for CTI: although written for legal accountability, the same discipline makes CTI findings reviewable: a plan tied to a requirement, a preservation record, explicit verification and a clear separation between what was observed and what was inferred.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The leak-site download

**Context:** after a ransomware group named a fictional Indian hospital on its leak site, a Nilgiri analyst downloaded the published archive "to check whether our policyholders were affected" and shared it on an internal drive.

**What happened:** the archive contained patients' medical records and identity numbers. Within days it was copied to three teams; one copy was attached to an e-mail to a vendor. Legal counsel stopped the work.

**Response:** the copies were deleted under a documented procedure; the OSINT policy was changed so that leak-site material is examined only by a designated team in an isolated environment, after legal approval, with minimisation and a strict retention limit; and a process was agreed to notify affected organisations through proper channels.

**Lessons:** "publicly available" stolen data still carries privacy, legal and ethical obligations. Plan, approve, minimise, restrict, record.
