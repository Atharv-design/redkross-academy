## About this module

Open-source intelligence is intelligence produced from publicly available information. "Publicly available" does not mean "free of rules": how you collect, what you keep, how you judge a source and how you protect the people whose data you touch all determine whether the result is reliable, lawful and ethical. This module gives you a disciplined method: plan the inquiry, collect passively, preserve with a verifiable record, grade and corroborate sources, minimise personal data, and stay within legal and ethical boundaries. The method draws on the Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations, published by the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights and the Human Rights Center at UC Berkeley [R1].

## Why it matters

Unverified open-source claims spread fast and can mislead decisions; in the lab, the most specific claim in the dataset (that a named state agency runs the campaign) comes from the least reliable source. Careless collection can also harm third parties, breach platform terms or the law, and expose the analyst or the organisation. A defensible method protects the consumer, the analyst and the people in the data.

## Where it is used

CTI teams researching campaigns and infrastructure, brand-protection and fraud teams, incident responders checking leak sites for stolen data, due-diligence and third-party risk teams.

## Learning objectives

1. Define passive and active collection and choose collection methods that stay passive.
2. Plan and run an open-source inquiry in phases from preparation to analysis.
3. Preserve collected material with hashes, timestamps and a tamper-evident collection log.
4. Grade sources and information separately with the Admiralty system and test corroboration and independence.
5. Apply data minimisation and the principle of minimising harm.
6. Explain the main legal boundaries for OSINT in India and where to seek advice.
