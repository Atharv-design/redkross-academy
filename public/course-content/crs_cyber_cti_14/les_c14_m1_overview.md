## About this module

Threat intelligence is not a feed of bad addresses. It is analysed, evaluated information that answers a decision-maker's question in time to change what they do. This module lays the foundations used in every later module: who the consumers are and what they need (requirements), how intelligence work is organised (the lifecycle and collection planning), and how to state judgments so that readers know both how likely something is and how much to trust the assessment. You will work with the requirements of Nilgiri Mutual, a fictional Indian insurer, and lint draft judgments against a published estimative standard.

## Why it matters

Without requirements, intelligence teams collect what is easy rather than what is needed, and consumers stop reading. Without calibrated language, "may" and "could" leave the reader to guess. The US Intelligence Community's analytic standard ICD 203 prescribes a fixed likelihood scale and requires that confidence in a judgment be expressed separately from its likelihood [R1]. Those two disciplines - requirements and calibration - separate intelligence from noise.

## Where it is used

Cyber threat intelligence (CTI) teams in banks, insurers, telecoms and government; SOCs that consume intelligence; risk committees that commission strategic assessments; sector sharing communities (ISACs).

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish data, information and intelligence, and the strategic, operational, tactical and technical levels.
2. Write priority and specific intelligence requirements with essential elements of information.
3. Describe the intelligence lifecycle and build a collection plan with gaps.
4. Express judgments with a likelihood scale and a separate confidence statement, and distinguish information from assumptions.
5. Recognise common cognitive biases and apply structured analytic techniques to counter them.
6. Measure the value of intelligence through consumer feedback.
