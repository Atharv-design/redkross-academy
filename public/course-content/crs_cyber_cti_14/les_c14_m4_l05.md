**Attribution has levels:**

1. technical clustering: these events belong together;
2. association with a known intrusion set;
3. identification of the organisation or individuals;
4. state or sponsor responsibility.

Each level needs more and different evidence. Organisations usually work at level 1, sometimes level 2; levels 3 and 4 typically rely on intelligence, legal or law-enforcement sources most defenders do not have.

**Why it is hard:**

- techniques are shared: the lab's overlap test (deep dive) found six ATT&CK groups that use all eight parent techniques of MONSOON LANTERN, and sixteen that use at least seven;
- tools are bought, leaked and shared;
- infrastructure is rented, compromised or shared;
- adversaries plant false flags (CS1);
- public reporting is incomplete, so a group's ATT&CK profile reflects what has been published about it, not everything it does.

For defence, attribution often matters less than behaviour: blocking, detection and hardening are the same whichever group is responsible. Attribution helps when it predicts something useful (likely next targets or techniques) and when consumers need it for legal or strategic decisions - then it must be well supported.
