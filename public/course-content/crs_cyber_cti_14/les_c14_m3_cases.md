## SUNBURST's DNS infrastructure, 2020 Context: on 13 December 2020 FireEye (now part of Google Cloud's Mandiant) published its analysis of the SUNBURST backdoor delivered through SolarWinds Orion updates [C14-M03-R3]. What the infrastructure looked like: the backdoor resolved subdomains of avsvmcloud[.]com generated by an algorithm that encoded a victim identifier and the victim's domain; DNS responses with CNAME records handed selected victims to command-and-control servers; the malware could stay dormant for up to two weeks. A 2022 update merged the activity cluster UNC2452 into APT29. Lessons: - Infrastructure analysis can operate at the level of DNS behaviour, not only indicator lists: the subdomain pattern itself was the signature. - Passive DNS and resolver logs let many organisations check retrospectively whether they had resolved the domain, and when. - Attribution came much later and was revised; infrastructure intelligence was actionable long before it (Module 4).



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The address that hosted a partner

**Context:** a regional broker shared a list of phishing addresses with Nilgiri Mutual (fictional). The SOC blocked all of them at the firewall the same afternoon.

**What happened:** one address was shared hosting that also served the booking site of a Nilgiri claims-repair partner. For two days claim appointments failed; the help desk logged 140 complaints before anyone connected them to the block.

**Response:** the block was replaced by a domain-level block for the lure; the SOC added a check of passive DNS fan-out and CDN ranges before any address block; partner domains were added to an allowlist with an owner.

**Lessons:** an address is not an identity. Check fan-out and context before blocking; prefer the most specific indicator.
