## Clearing the logs - why forwarding matters Context: across many intrusions documented by incident responders and in MITRE ATT&CK (Indicator Removal: Clear Windows Event Logs, T1070.001), attackers who reach a domain controller attempt to clear the Security event log to destroy the record of their logons and account changes. If the only copy of the telemetry is the local log on the compromised DC, clearing it blinds the defender [C13-M06-R2]. Why it matters for this module: it is the reason identity events must be FORWARDED off the DC in near-real time to a SIEM or log service the attacker does not control (L03). A detection is only as good as the durability of the events it reads. Lessons: forward DC security events off-host; alert on log-clearing (event 1102) itself; treat the loss of expected telemetry as a signal, not just an absence.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The 3 a.m. burst Context: Sahyadri Logistics (fictional) receives an alert from its authentication monitor: a burst of failed logons for the account "asha" at 03:00, followed by a success. What happened: the analyst scoped the alert (one account, one source, a tight window), checked the baseline (asha normally logs on during business hours from a known workstation), and corroborated (no lockout, the success used AES from the same source). It turned out to be asha's own laptop retrying a stale saved password after a change - benign - but the investigation was correct and documented. Response: the analyst recorded the benign conclusion, added no blanket suppression (which would have hidden a real burst), and noted that a success from a NEW source after a burst would have been escalated. Lessons: investigate with scope-baseline-corroborate-decide-record; a benign result still deserves a recorded conclusion; do not suppress the rule to silence one benign case.
