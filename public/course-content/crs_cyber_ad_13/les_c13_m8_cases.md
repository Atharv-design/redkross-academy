## AD recovery planning as the ultimate capstone Context: after major identity compromises (the NotPetya-class events of Module 4 and numerous ransomware intrusions), organisations and Microsoft developed detailed Active Directory forest recovery guidance, because rebuilding trust after a domain controller or krbtgt compromise is complex and must be planned in advance [C13-M08-R2]. A hardening project is incomplete without a tested recovery plan: how to rebuild DCs from clean media, rotate krbtgt (twice, Module 5), reset trusts, and restore service. Why it matters for the capstone: hardening reduces the chance of compromise; recovery planning limits the damage when it happens anyway. A mature audit report addresses both - the controls in place AND the ability to recover. Lessons: pair hardening with a tested forest-recovery plan; rehearse krbtgt rotation and DC rebuild; treat recovery as a first-class control, not an afterthought.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The ninety-day report Context: a new identity-security engineer joins Sahyadri Logistics (fictional) and is asked for a ninety-day plan to improve AD security. What happened: using the course method, they built the assessment (Module 7), hardened the top findings (closing the Kerberoastable account), and produced a report: scope, method, before-findings, actions with evidence, after-findings with closure, and a forward plan (LAPS, PAWs, a second DC, a tested recovery runbook) with costs and owners. Response: leadership approved the forward plan because the report proved quick wins already delivered (evidence of closure) and framed the projects in risk terms. The engineer had turned a vague mandate into a funded programme. Lessons: deliver quick wins with evidence first to earn trust; frame projects as risk reduction with owners and costs; a clear report is how identity security gets funded.
