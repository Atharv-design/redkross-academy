## Group Policy as a weak link - guidance and the SYSVOL password problem

**Context:** for years, a common and damaging misconfiguration was storing local administrator passwords in Group Policy Preferences. The password was encrypted with a key Microsoft had published (the "GPP cpassword" issue, addressed by MS14-025), and SYSVOL is readable by every authenticated user - so any domain user could read and decrypt those local admin passwords across the estate [R4]. Why it matters for this module: it shows that Group Policy is not only a way to push settings but also a store that every user can read, and that its contents are security-sensitive. It is also why Microsoft built the Local Administrator Password Solution (LAPS) to manage local admin passwords properly (Module 4).

**Lessons:** never store secrets in Group Policy; treat SYSVOL as readable by all users; use LAPS for local administrator passwords; keep DCs patched.

## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The group that grew Context: Sahyadri Logistics (fictional) has a group "Finance-Share-Full" that was created years ago to grant full control of a finance file share. Over time, help-desk staff added people to it "to fix access quickly". What happened: an access review found 38 members, including contractors who had left, staff who had changed roles, and one service account. No one owned the group or reviewed it. A single compromised member account would have had full control of all finance data. Response: the team assigned an owner, re-based the group on the AGDLP pattern (a global role group feeding a domain-local permission group), removed stale and inappropriate members, set a quarterly review, and logged the changes. Membership fell to 9. Lessons: every privileged or sensitive group needs an owner and a review cadence; use AGDLP so role and permission are separable; access granted "to fix it quickly" is rarely removed unless a process removes it.
