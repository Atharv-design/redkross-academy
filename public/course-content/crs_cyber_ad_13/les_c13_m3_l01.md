An organisational unit (OU) is a container inside a domain used to organise objects for two purposes: to delegate administration (grant a help-desk team rights over only the OUs they manage) and to target Group Policy (link a workstation baseline to the Workstations OU). OUs are not security groups - you do not put users in an OU to give them permissions; you put them there to manage them. A good OU design mirrors how the organisation is administered, not its org chart. In the lab you will create OUs that express a tiered administration model: Tier0, Tier1, Tier2, ServiceAccounts, Workstations and Servers (Module 4 explains the tiers). Delegation is done with access-control entries on the OU, following least privilege: grant the specific rights needed (reset passwords in this OU) rather than broad administrative group membership.
