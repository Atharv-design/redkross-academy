When an account authenticates to a computer, credential material for that account can end up in that computer's memory (tickets, hashes, cached secrets). If the computer is compromised, those credentials are exposed. This is the engine of lateral movement and privilege escalation: an attacker who compromises a workstation harvests whatever credentials are present there, then reuses them to reach more systems. The fatal pattern is a HIGH-PRIVILEGE account used on a LOW-TRUST system: a domain admin who logs on to an ordinary, internet-exposed workstation exposes domain-admin credentials on exactly the kind of machine attackers compromise first. Containment means ensuring that the credentials present on a system are only ever as powerful as the system's trust level - so a breach stays contained to that level.
