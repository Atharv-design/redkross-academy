## CircleCI, December 2022 - secrets stored in a platform -------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: CircleCI's incident report on the security incident it announced on 4 January 2023 stated that on 16 December 2022 malware was deployed to an engineer's laptop and was not detected by antivirus software. The malware stole a valid, 2FA-backed SSO session cookie, allowing impersonation of the employee. On 22 December 2022 customer data including environment variables, tokens and keys was exfiltrated; although data was encrypted at rest, encryption keys were extracted from a running process, potentially enabling access to the encrypted data. CircleCI urged customers to rotate all secrets and recommended OIDC tokens to avoid storing long-lived credentials [C11-M07-R1]. Workload lessons: - Every secret stored in a CI/CD platform is exposed if the platform is breached; prefer OIDC federation and short-lived credentials. - Keep an inventory of secrets and where they are used so they can be rotated quickly. - Session cookies are credentials too: device security, token binding and session lifetime matter for administrators. - Encryption at rest does not protect data from a process that holds the keys (Module 4).



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The function that could read every bucket ----------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. A Tamarind Lambda that resized images had an execution role with `s3:*` on `"*"` copied from a tutorial. A dependency in the function had a vulnerability that allowed arbitrary file reads in the function's environment. Investigation found no misuse, but the role could have read payroll exports. The fix: a role limited to two buckets and two actions, dependency scanning in CI, and an alert on S3 access by function roles outside their buckets.
