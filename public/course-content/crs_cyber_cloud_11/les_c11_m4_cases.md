## Toyota Connected, 2023 - a decade of exposure from one setting ------------------------------------------------------------------------ Context: In 2023 Toyota disclosed that a misconfigured cloud environment had exposed vehicle location data of about 2.15 million customers in Japan from November 2013 to April 2023. The company attributed the issue to human error and said it had found no evidence of misuse [C11-M04-R3]. Data protection lessons: - Exposure persists until someone looks: continuous posture checks and alerts on access-setting changes shorten exposure from years to hours (Modules 5-6). - Location data is personal data; classification should have required private-only access and customer-managed controls. - Access logs are needed to answer "was it accessed?" - enable them before an incident. - Default-deny guardrails (account-level public access blocks, Azure Policy) prevent one human error from becoming an exposure.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The SAS link in the ticket ----------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. A Tamarind engineer pasted a storage account SAS URL with read/list permissions and one-year expiry into a vendor support ticket. Months later the ticketing system was itself breached. Response: account keys rotated (invalidating key-signed SAS), user delegation SAS adopted, an expiration policy set to one day, and DLP rules added to the ticketing system to detect SAS URLs.
