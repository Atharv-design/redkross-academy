Because business logic flaws are about intended behaviour, finding them requires understanding the workflow and asking "what if?": what if I repeat this step, skip it, reorder it, send an unexpected value (negative, zero, very large), or call endpoints directly out of sequence? Testing is manual and model-driven: map the intended workflow and its rules (who may do what, in what order, with what limits), then probe each assumption. Defences are design-level: enforce every business rule on the SERVER (quantities, sequence, limits, prices looked up server-side), make state transitions explicit and validated, apply idempotency and limits (a coupon is marked used; a step cannot be skipped), and never trust client-supplied business values. The lab's fix for the hidden-field price - look the price up server-side and ignore any client price - is the archetype.
