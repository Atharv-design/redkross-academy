CVSS gives a consistent, reproducible severity, but prioritisation should add context: which findings are internet-reachable; which touch the most sensitive data; which are easy to exploit; and which have known exploitation in the wild (CISA KEV) or high exploitation likelihood (EPSS), as Course 05 covers. A base-score Medium on a crown-jewel, internet-facing endpoint may outrank a base-score High on an internal, low-value one. The report should present the CVSS ranking and note where business context adjusts it. In the lab, the ranking runs from SQL injection (9.8, full database compromise and auth bypass) down to missing security headers (3.1), which is a sensible fix order; context would, for a real app, weight the access-control and injection findings even more because they expose customer data directly.
