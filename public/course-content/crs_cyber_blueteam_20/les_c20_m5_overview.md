## About this module

Detections wait for known patterns; hunters go looking for the unknown. Threat hunting is a proactive, human-led search through telemetry for adversary activity that existing detections missed. This module teaches hunting as a disciplined method. You will write testable hypotheses, state the data each hunt needs and check that the data exists, analyse it with stacking and baselines, validate the result, and turn every hunt into lasting improvements, whether it finds an attacker or not.

## Why it matters

Joint guidance on living-off-the-land (LOTL) techniques from CISA, the NSA, the FBI and partners notes that many organisations lack established baselines. That makes it hard to tell legitimate behaviour from malicious behaviour and to "conduct behavioral analytics, anomaly detection, and proactive hunting" [R1]. Hunting builds those baselines, and finds what rules miss in the meantime.

## Where it is used

Dedicated hunt teams in large SOCs, managed detection and response providers, incident-response scoping ("are they anywhere else?"), and periodic hunts driven by new threat intelligence.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish hypothesis-driven, baseline and model-assisted hunts.
2. Write testable, scoped hunt hypotheses from intelligence and the threat model.
3. Specify and verify the data requirements of a hunt.
4. Apply stacking, rarity, baselining and time-window correlation.
5. Validate hunt results, including null results.
6. Convert hunt findings into detections, telemetry fixes and reports.
