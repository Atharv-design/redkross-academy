## SUNBURST and DNS, 2020 Context: on 13 December 2020 FireEye (now Mandiant, part of Google Cloud) described the SUNBURST backdoor delivered through trojanised SolarWinds Orion updates [C20-M03-R3]. After a dormant period of up to two weeks, the malware resolved subdomains of avsvmcloud[.]com. The subdomains were generated by combining a victim identifier with an encoding of the local machine's domain name, and CNAME responses pointed it to command-and-control domains. A May 2022 update to the post attributes the activity to APT29. Lessons: DNS logs recorded the earliest network sign of compromise, including the victim's own domain name encoded in queries. Organisations that kept resolver logs could scope their exposure retrospectively once indicators were published, so retention (Module 1) decided who could answer "were we affected?". Servers that run management software should have tightly restricted egress, which turns unexpected DNS and connections into high-fidelity signals.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The clockwork printer This scenario is fictional. A Tamarind analyst wrote a regularity analytic and found 40 perfectly regular pairs on day one. Thirty-eight were printers reporting toner levels to the vendor and two were NTP. The analyst allowlisted them by destination and purpose with an owner, rather than raising the CV threshold, which would have hidden the real WS-07 beacon with its jitter of plus or minus 12 seconds. Lesson: tune by understanding the benign population, not by loosening the math.
