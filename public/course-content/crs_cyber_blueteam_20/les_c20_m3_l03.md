Automated software connects on schedules. Software that checks in with a remote controller often does so at a fixed interval with small random "jitter". Group flows by (source, destination, port) and measure the gaps between connections. The coefficient of variation (CV = standard deviation / mean of the gaps) is near 0 for clockwork connections and larger for human browsing. Tamarind's WS-07 connected to 198.51.100.23:443 83 times with a mean gap of 297 seconds and a CV of 0.083, with a median of 452 bytes out per connection.

Treat regularity as a lead, not a verdict. Update agents, monitoring probes, NTP and mail clients are also regular. Allowlist known services deliberately, and combine regularity with other evidence (a new destination, low byte counts, matching DNS anomalies, and the process that owns the connection from Sysmon Event ID 3). Volume analytics matter too. The largest transfers in Tamarind's data are a nightly internal backup of 900 MB per flow over SMB at 01:00. The 312 MB outbound flow from WS-07 at 12:31 to the same external address is far more interesting, although it is smaller.
