## The EU Artificial Intelligence Act, 2024 - assessment becomes an expectation ------------------------------------------------------------------ Context: EUR-Lex publishes Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act), published in the Official Journal (OJ L) on 12 July 2024; EUR-Lex also shows consolidated versions (one dated 27 July 2026) [C18-M08-R1]. Lessons for assessors: - Organisations need to determine, with legal advice, whether and how their AI systems fall within the regulation's scope and categories. - Whatever the legal outcome, documented risk assessment, testing evidence, logging and human oversight are the practical artefacts that regulators, customers and auditors ask for. - The capstone report format - scope, method, failure paths, evidence, residual risk - produces those artefacts. - Laws are consolidated and amended; check the current version and its application dates rather than relying on summaries.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The approval board ----------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. Banyan's AI governance board received two reports for competing support assistants. One said "tested and secure". The other listed five failure paths with evidence, intervals, unit-tested controls, a utility gap and a recommendation not to launch until a 60-run evaluation passed. The board approved the second team's plan, funded the fixes, and adopted its report format as the standard for AI approvals.
