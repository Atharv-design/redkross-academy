## About this module

**AI applications are data-hungry:** models are trained or fine-tuned on large datasets, retrieval indexes copy documents into new stores, and every conversation is new data. This module covers how sensitive information is exposed through AI systems (OWASP LLM02:2025), the different risks of training data versus retrieval data, how to govern data across the AI lifecycle, and the privacy obligations that apply. The lab audits BanyanBot's knowledge base before indexing and measures cross-customer disclosure with and without retrieval controls.

## Why it matters

In late March 2023 Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, ordered OpenAI to temporarily stop processing Italian users' personal information while it investigated; ChatGPT returned in Italy on 28 April 2023 after OpenAI added information about how training data is collected and used, an objection form for EU users and age verification at sign-up (case study). Privacy regulators treat AI data processing like any other processing.

## Where it is used

Privacy impact assessments for AI features, data protection officer reviews, data classification for AI, retention policies for prompts and outputs, vendor terms for AI services, DLP for AI tools.

## Learning objectives

1. Identify the routes by which AI systems expose sensitive data.
2. Compare the risks of data in training, fine-tuning, retrieval and conversation logs.
3. Apply retrieval access control and output redaction.
4. Audit data before indexing or training.
5. Govern AI data: minimisation, purpose, retention, deletion, vendor terms.
6. Map AI data processing to privacy obligations.
