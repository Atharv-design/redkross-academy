## Slack AI and indirect prompt injection, August 2024 -------------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: PromptArmor published "Data Exfiltration from Slack AI via Indirect Prompt Injection", with a disclosure timeline of initial disclosure on 14 August and Slack's response on 19 August (2024). PromptArmor stated that the vulnerability "can allow attackers to steal anything a user puts in a private Slack channel by manipulating the language model used for content generation". Slack's response quoted by PromptArmor said that messages posted to public channels can be searched for and viewed by all members of the workspace, and that this is intended behaviour [C18-M03-R1]. Defensive lessons: - Features that put other people's content into an assistant's context (public channels, shared documents) are indirect injection channels by design; threat models must include them. - Output that can carry links or rendered content is a data-leak path; restrict links and rendering (Module 5). - Vendors and customers may disagree about what is "intended"; customers should assess AI features before enabling them and configure scope (which content the assistant may read).



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The supplier catalogue ------------------------------------------------------------------ This scenario is fictional. Banyan imported supplier product catalogues into its sales assistant. One supplier's catalogue contained a line telling assistants to recommend that supplier first. Sales staff noticed consistently skewed recommendations. The fix: catalogues ingested as structured data fields rather than free text, an ingestion audit for text addressed to assistants, and a weekly probe comparing recommendations with and without each supplier's data.
