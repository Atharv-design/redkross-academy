## Air Canada's chatbot, February 2024 - owning what the AI says ------------------------------------------------------------------------ Context: CTV News reported on 14 February 2024 that "a B.C. tribunal found the airline was responsible for inaccurate information given to a traveller by its chatbot" [C18-M05-R1]. The case concerned information about fares that the traveller relied upon. Lessons: - Output is the organisation's statement; reliance creates liability. - Policy and price answers should come from authoritative, structured sources, with the chatbot linking to the policy rather than paraphrasing it. - Consequential answers need clear limits and a route to a human. - Accuracy testing is part of AI assurance (Module 7).



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The SQL assistant ----------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. Banyan's analytics team let an assistant turn questions into SQL and run them with the reporting database's owner account. A user's question produced a query that dropped a temporary table other reports depended on. The fix: a read-only role, an allowlist of views, query parsing that rejects anything but SELECT, row limits, a review step for new query patterns, and full query logging.
