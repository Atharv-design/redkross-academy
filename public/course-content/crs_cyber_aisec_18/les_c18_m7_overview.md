## About this module

AI red teaming is authorised, adversarially-minded evaluation of an AI system to find failures before others do. For defenders it is a measurement discipline: define what must not happen, design safe probes that would reveal it, run them enough times to say something meaningful, and report risk in terms decision-makers can act on. This module covers planning and rules of engagement, safe probe design with canaries and simulated tools, statistics for probabilistic systems, regression suites, and risk reporting. The lab turns BanyanBot's probe results into a risk report with confidence intervals.

## Why it matters

Microsoft's AI Red Team published "Lessons From Red Teaming 100 Generative AI Products" in January 2025, describing work on over 100 generative AI products and lessons including "AI red teaming is not safety benchmarking" and "The work of securing AI systems will never be complete" (case study). Evaluation is continuous engineering work, not a one-off certificate.

## Where it is used

Pre-release assurance for AI features, vendor AI assessments, internal red teams and purple teams (Course 20), regulatory and customer assurance, and continuous evaluation pipelines.

## Learning objectives

1. Plan an AI evaluation with objectives, scope, authorisation and rules of engagement.
2. Design safe, benign probes with objective pass/fail checks.
3. Run probabilistic evaluations with seeds and enough repetitions, and interpret uncertainty.
4. Combine model-driven probes with deterministic control tests.
5. Build a regression suite for continuous evaluation.
6. Report AI risk with evidence, uncertainty and recommendations.
