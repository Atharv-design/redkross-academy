## Lessons from red teaming 100 generative AI products, January 2025 ------------------------------------------------------------------------ Context: "Lessons From Red Teaming 100 Generative AI Products" by B. Bullwinkel, A. Minnich, S. Chawla and colleagues including M. Russinovich was submitted to arXiv on 13 January 2025. It draws on red teaming of over 100 generative AI products at Microsoft. Among its lessons: "You don't have to compute gradients to break an AI system", "AI red teaming is not safety benchmarking", and "The work of securing AI systems will never be complete" [C18-M07-R1]. Lessons for defenders: - Simple, system-level probes (like BanyanBot's) find real failures; you do not need advanced techniques to evaluate risk. - Benchmarks of models are not a substitute for testing the deployed system with its retrieval, tools and UI. - Evaluation must be continuous because systems and threats change.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): "We tested it once" ------------------------------------------------------------------------ This scenario is fictional. A Banyan team ran each probe once before launch, saw no failures and declared the assistant secure. A month later, after a model update, customers reported the assistant showing internal reference text. Re-running the same probes 30 times showed a 20% leak rate. The team adopted multi-run evaluation with intervals, a release gate on model updates, and production canary monitoring.
