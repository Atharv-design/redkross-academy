## About this module

The capstone brings the course together in one end-to-end assessment of a controlled AI application - the BanyanBot bench on your own computer. You will scope the assessment, collect evidence with the course tools, document failure paths (the "attack paths" of the blueprint, written from the defender's side: how untrusted input could travel through the system to an impact, and which control stops it at each step), propose mitigations, and report residual risk. This module teaches the method and works through one reference path from the lab; Part C (C4) sets the full capstone.

## Why it matters

Regulators now expect evidence. The EU's Artificial Intelligence Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of 13 June 2024, was published in the Official Journal on 12 July 2024 (case study). Whatever the jurisdiction, an assessment that documents how an AI system can fail, what controls exist and how they were tested is becoming a normal expectation of customers, auditors and regulators.

## Where it is used

Pre-launch security assessments of AI features, vendor AI due diligence, internal audit of AI systems, governance board approvals and post-incident reviews.

## Learning objectives

1. Scope an AI application assessment with authorisation and criteria.
2. Collect and organise evidence from probes, unit tests, audits and provenance checks.
3. Document failure paths from source to impact with controls at each hop.
4. Prioritise mitigations and state residual risk.
5. Write an assessment report for engineers and decision-makers.
6. Hand over to continuous evaluation and governance.
