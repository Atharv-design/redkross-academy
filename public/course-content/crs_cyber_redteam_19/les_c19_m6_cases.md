## Beaconing as a detection anchor Context: network defenders and ATT&CK (T1071 Application Layer Protocol) note that C2 over common protocols is designed to blend in, but that its BEACONING - regular, machine-like check-ins to a destination - is a reliable behavioural indicator. Detection methods that find near-constant intervals to a destination, combined with destination reputation and egress control, are a mainstay of catching and cutting C2; this is why egress filtering and proxy inspection are emphasised defensively [C19-M06-R1][C19-M06-R2]. Why it matters for this module: it shows C2 is detectable and containable at the network boundary through behaviour, not protocol blocking - exactly what the lab maps. Lessons: detect C2 by beaconing behaviour; contain it with egress filtering and proxy inspection; block known indicators and hunt for reuse.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The beacon to the sink Context: in a fictional engagement for Nallamala Energy, the emulation produces a C2 beacon (T1071) to a lab sink inside the isolated range. What happened: mapping the technique (attackmap.py, C2 filter) defined the detection (regular beacon to a single destination) and containment (egress filtering, proxy inspection, block the indicator). When emulated, the proxy DID alert on the periodic beacon - a detection success - and the team confirmed that egress filtering would have blocked a real external destination. Response: the team recommended tightening egress (deny-by-default outbound), proxy inspection for all web egress, and a beacon-detection analytic, then validated by re-emulating the beacon. No real external server was ever contacted. Lessons: emulate only the C2 indicator to a lab sink; detect the beacon; contain with egress and proxy controls.
