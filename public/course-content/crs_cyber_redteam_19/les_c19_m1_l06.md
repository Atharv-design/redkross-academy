Authorised testing is lawful; the same acts without authorisation are crimes. In India, unauthorised access to, downloading from, or causing damage to a computer resource engages the Information Technology Act, 2000 - for example s.43 and s.66 (unauthorised access/damage), s.66C (identity), s.66D (impersonation) and s.66F (cyber terrorism for the most serious cases) - and personal data is governed by the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. The ethical duties follow: act only within authorisation and scope; do no harm; protect any data encountered; be honest in reporting; and respect the deconfliction and stop rules. Professional codes (and NIST SP 800-115's emphasis on planning and rules) reflect the same. This is not legal advice; obtain qualified counsel for your facts. The practical consequence: the RoE and authorisation are not paperwork - they are what makes the work legitimate.
