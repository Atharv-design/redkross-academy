## Valid Accounts and the limits of signature detection Context: ATT&CK documents Valid Accounts (T1078) as a technique that spans Initial Access, Persistence, Privilege Escalation and more, precisely because using legitimate credentials is hard to distinguish from normal activity. ATT&CK and defensive guidance stress that detecting it relies on behavioural analytics over authentication telemetry - anomalous source, time, or account behaviour - and on containment such as MFA and privilege tiering, rather than on signatures [C19-M04-R1]. Why it matters for this module: it shows why defensive TELEMETRY and behavioural detection, not signatures, are the answer to identity abuse - the core of this module's mapping. Lessons: collect rich authentication telemetry; detect identity abuse behaviourally; contain with MFA, tiering and least privilege.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The quiet service account Context: in a fictional engagement for Vindhya Manufacturing, the emulation uses a lab service account (Valid Accounts, T1078) from an unusual host at an odd hour. What happened: mapping the technique (attackmap.py) showed the required telemetry (authentication logs) and detection (anomalous logon for a service account). When the behaviour was emulated in the range, the SIEM did alert - but a follow-on Account Discovery step (T1087) produced no alert, revealing a telemetry gap in directory-read monitoring. Response: the team recommended behavioural detection for service-account logon anomalies (already present) and, for the gap, directory-read monitoring plus least privilege - each paired with containment (MFA, tiered admin). The gaps feed Module 7. Lessons: map every identity technique to telemetry and a detection; the gaps are the findings; pair detection with containment.
