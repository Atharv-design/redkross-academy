## About this module

Lateral movement is how an adversary spreads from an initial foothold to their real objective, hopping between hosts and accounts. This module treats lateral movement

**CONCEPTUALLY and defensively:** the trust relationships it abuses, how segmentation validation limits it, and the detection opportunities it creates. You will use the lab's ATT&CK map, filtered to the Lateral Movement tactic, to turn those techniques into detection and containment.

## Why it matters

Most damaging intrusions depend on lateral movement: the first foothold is rarely the goal. For defenders, lateral movement is both a risk and an OPPORTUNITY - each hop crosses a trust boundary and touches authentication and network telemetry, giving multiple chances to detect and contain. Understanding the trust relationships it abuses, validating segmentation, and building detections for the hops is how an engagement helps an organisation stop an intrusion before it reaches its objective.

## Where it is used

Segmentation and zero-trust validation; identity-security and tiering reviews; detection engineering for lateral movement (ties to Course 07 segmentation and Course 13 Active Directory); and purple-team exercises.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain lateral movement and the trust relationships it abuses.
2. Explain segmentation and its validation.
3. Explain the lateral-movement ATT&CK techniques (T1021, T1550).
4. Identify the detection opportunities each hop creates.
5. Explain containment through segmentation and credential hygiene.
6. Keep lateral-movement testing conceptual, isolated and safe.
