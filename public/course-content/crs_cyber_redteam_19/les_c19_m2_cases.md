## ATT&CK and published adversary-emulation plans Context: MITRE ATT&CK has become the common language for describing adversary behaviour, and MITRE's Center for Threat-Informed Defense and the ATT&CK team publish ADVERSARY EMULATION PLANS - structured sequences of ATT&CK techniques representing how specific threat actors operate - so defenders can emulate them and test detection. Organisations use ATT&CK to plan threat-informed emulations, map their detection coverage, and communicate findings in a shared vocabulary [C19-M02-R1][C19-M02-R2]. Why it matters for this module: it shows emulation as a disciplined, threat-informed practice built on a shared knowledge base, not ad hoc attacking - exactly what the lab's ATT&CK-mapped plan models. Lessons: build emulation from a threat profile and ATT&CK; map every technique to a detection; use the shared language so red and blue align.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): Emulating the right adversary Context: a fictional bank, Deccan Financial, faces credential-theft and lateral- movement threats. A red team plans an emulation. What happened: instead of arbitrary attacks, the team built a threat-informed plan of ATT&CK techniques the relevant adversary uses - Active Scanning (T1595), Valid Accounts (T1078), Remote Services (T1021), Use Alternate Authentication Material (T1550) and Application Layer Protocol C2 (T1071) - each bounded to the isolated range and mapped to the detection it should trigger. Validating the plan (emulation_plan.py) confirmed every step was in scope, safety-bounded and detection- mapped before anything ran. Response: the exercise emulated exactly the behaviours that mattered to the bank's threat model, and because each was tied to a detection, the blue team could measure coverage (Module 7). Lessons: emulate the relevant adversary, bound every step, and pair each technique with the detection it tests.
