## About this module

Inside the authorised scope, an engagement maps the infrastructure - assets and identities - validates findings in a controlled way, and manages evidence rigorously. This module covers asset and identity mapping, controlled validation, and evidence management. You will use the lab's scope-enforcement check (which refuses any out-of-scope target) and the tamper-evident evidence log (which keeps a verifiable chain of custody).

## Why it matters

An engagement is only credible if it stays strictly in scope and its findings are backed by trustworthy evidence. Asset and identity mapping orients the work; controlled validation confirms a finding with the minimum necessary action (no collateral damage); and evidence management - timestamps, operator attribution and integrity - makes the findings defensible and the report trustworthy. These disciplines, not exploitation, are what make an assessment professional.

## Where it is used

The discovery and validation phases of penetration tests and red team engagements (NIST SP 800-115 discovery); scope control; and evidence and chain-of-custody practices shared with digital forensics (Course 09).

## Learning objectives

1. Explain asset and identity mapping within scope.
2. Explain scope enforcement and fail-safe out-of-scope handling.
3. Explain controlled validation (minimal, non-destructive proof).
4. Explain evidence management and chain of custody.
5. Use a scope check and a tamper-evident evidence log.
6. Explain why integrity and attribution matter for findings.

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** Modules 1-2; Course 09 (forensics/evidence) helpful.
