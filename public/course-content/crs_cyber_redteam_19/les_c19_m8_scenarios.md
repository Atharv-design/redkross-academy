## Scenario 1

You must brief executives and engineers on the same engagement.

**Task:** Explain what each report contains and why they differ.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> The executive report is short, jargon-light and for decision-makers: scope and authorisation, the detection coverage (e.g. 4/7), what it means for risk, and the top few recommendations (the highest-value gaps) to resource. The technical report is detailed and for engineers/the blue team: each emulated technique with its ATT&CK mapping and DETECTED/GAP status, containment, the retest list, and evidence references. They differ because the audiences make different decisions - funding/risk versus engineering remediation.
>
> **Lesson:** write two reports for two audiences from the same evidenced engagement.

## Scenario 2

A finding in the draft report has no supporting evidence.

**Task:** Explain what traceability requires.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Traceability requires every statement to trace to engagement evidence - the emulation plan (what was done), the telemetry (what was seen) and the evidence log (chain of custody). A finding with no supporting evidence cannot be substantiated, so it must be evidenced or removed. This keeps the report trustworthy and defensible and protects both parties.
>
> **Lesson:** no evidence, not in the report.

## Scenario 3

The exercise detected only 4 of 7 techniques.

**Task:** Explain how to report this honestly and usefully.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> **Report it as measured:** state that 4 of 7 emulated techniques were detected (57% coverage), name the three that were missed, and make closing those gaps the top recommendations with a retest path. Do not overstate or hide the gaps; note the limitation that the exercise covered the planned techniques. This honest, prioritised framing is what lets the organisation improve.
>
> **Lesson:** report coverage honestly and turn the gaps into prioritised, retestable actions.
