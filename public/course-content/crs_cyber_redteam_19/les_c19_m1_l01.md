Authorisation is written permission, from a party entitled to grant it, to perform defined testing against defined systems in a defined window. It is the legal basis for the entire engagement - without it, the same actions are unlawful access. Authorisation names the authorising party (and their authority over the targets), references a signed agreement, and is specific. Objectives state WHAT the engagement is for:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| goal-based | test whether the organisation can detect and respond to a realistic adversary (red team / adversary emulation). |
| coverage-based | assess a defined set of systems for weaknesses (penetration test). |
| question-based | answer a specific question ("can an attacker reach the payment system from a phished laptop?"). |

Clear objectives shape scope, the threat profile to emulate (Module 2) and the success criteria. The lab's RoE validator treats a missing authorising party as a

**HIGH finding:** no authorisation, no engagement.
