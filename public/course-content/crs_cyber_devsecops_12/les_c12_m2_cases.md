## PyTorch nightly dependency, December 2022 - the forgotten trust boundary ------------------------------------------------------------------------ Context: PyTorch reported that Linux users who installed PyTorch-nightly with pip between 25 and 30 December 2022 received a malicious package named torchtriton from the public Python Package Index (PyPI), instead of the genuine dependency of the same name hosted on PyTorch's own nightly index. PyPI took precedence over the project's index. The malicious package included a binary that collected system information and files, including SSH configuration, and sent them out over DNS. PyTorch removed the dependency, renamed it pytorch-triton, registered a reserved dummy package on PyPI, and advised affected users to uninstall the packages and purge their pip cache. Users of stable releases were not affected [C12-M02-R3]. Threat-modelling lessons: - The boundary between the build and its package sources (B4 in the deep dive) is part of the system and belongs on the diagram. - A threat with high impact and seemingly low likelihood ("name collision across two indices") deserved an explicit decision. Prioritisation rules should not let impact-5 threats fall below the line. - Mitigations are design choices: a single trusted index, reserved names and hash-pinned dependencies.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The support shortcut -------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. Tamarind added an internal support tool that let agents change delivery addresses to help customers. No threat model was done, and the tool reused the agents' general "support" role. Months later, an agent's account was misused to redirect high-value parcels. A 30-minute STRIDE pass over the support tool, under E for elevation of privilege, would have raised the entry now in the register: "support staff can change delivery addresses". The mitigations would have been a separate role, dual approval and customer notification.
