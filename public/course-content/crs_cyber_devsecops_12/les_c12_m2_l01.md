The Threat Modeling Manifesto and much of the industry frame the practice around four questions, attributed to Adam Shostack's "4 Question Frame" [R1]:

1. What are we working on?           (model the system)
2. What can go wrong?                (identify threats)
3. What are we going to do about it? (decide mitigations)
4. Did we do a good enough job?      (validate and review)

**Practical guidance:**

- Keep sessions short (60-90 minutes) and focused on one feature or service.
- Invite the developers who will build it, an architect, a tester and a security engineer or champion.
- Record outputs in the repository next to the code: the diagram, the register and decisions.
- Revisit when the design changes significantly. A threat model is a living document.
