## Google BeyondCorp, 2014 - a published migration to zero trust ------------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: In "BeyondCorp: A New Approach to Enterprise Security" (R. Ward and B. Beyer, ;login:, Vol. 39, No. 6, 2014, pp. 6-11), Google described its programme in these terms: "We are removing the requirement for a privileged intranet and moving our corporate applications to the Internet" [C22-M08-R1]. The paper predates NIST SP 800-207 (August 2020) and is often cited as an early large-scale enterprise example of the approach the NIST publication later standardised. Architecture lessons: - A clear target statement ("no privileged intranet") gives every design decision a test. - Migration of a large enterprise is staged; a roadmap with dependencies and measurement is part of the architecture, not an afterthought. - Publishing the approach let others learn from it; reference architectures should be written to be understood outside the team. Note: this case uses only the paper's published abstract and bibliographic details; read the paper itself for its component descriptions.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The roadmap of products ------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. A logistics company's first zero trust roadmap was a list of six products with purchase dates. Eighteen months later, all six were deployed but the old VPN subnet was still trusted, PDP decisions were not logged centrally, and no-one could say whether risk had fallen. The rewrite started from a reference architecture and maturity assessment, tied each initiative to a validating requirement, and reported progress as the share of requirements validated. Two of the six products were retired as redundant.
