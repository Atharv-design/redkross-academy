## Ivanti Connect Secure, 2024 - when the gateway is the target ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: CISA, the FBI, MS-ISAC and partners in Australia, the UK, Canada and New Zealand published advisory AA24-060B (29 February 2024) on exploitation of Ivanti Connect Secure and Policy Secure gateways (CVE-2023-46805, CVE-2024-21887, CVE-2024-21893). CISA reported that Ivanti's internal and previous external Integrity Checker Tool failed to detect compromise, that actors cleaned up by overwriting and time-stomping files, and that CISA's research found an actor may be able to maintain root level persistence despite factory resets; the advisory advised assuming such persistence is possible and noted that collection on vendor-managed appliances may be limited [C22-M04-R1] (see Course 09 M04). Architecture lessons: - Remote-access gateways are tier-0-adjacent; reduce their exposure and privileges, and do not grant implicit trust to traffic behind them. - Segment what a compromised gateway can reach; require independent authentication to internal applications (Modules 2-3). - Prefer architectures where a single appliance compromise does not equal network-wide access (ZTNA per application, enclave gateways). - Plan detection and response for appliances you cannot instrument.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The flat depot network --------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. Each Tamarind depot had one flat network: label printers, CCTV, staff PCs and the dispatch server. A compromised printer management tool reached the dispatch server directly. The redesign created VLANs per device class, an enclave gateway for the dispatch server, default-deny inter-VLAN rules with an approved flow matrix, internet egress only via proxy, and a monthly automated connectivity test like the lab's.
