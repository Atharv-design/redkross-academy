## About this module

Security architecture is the discipline of shaping systems so that the security properties the business needs are built into their structure: where trust boundaries sit, how identities and data flow, which components enforce which decisions, and how the whole fails and recovers. A good architect starts from requirements rather than products, makes trust explicit, documents the design in views that different audiences can use, and records decisions so they can be revisited. This module covers those foundations.

## Why it matters

Architecture determines blast radius. The British Library's review of its 2023 ransomware attack concluded that its legacy infrastructure contributed to the severity of the impact and that the older network design allowed attackers wider access than a more modern design would have (case study). No amount of good operations fully compensates for an architecture that lets one foothold reach everything.

## Where it is used

Enterprise and solution architecture, design authorities and architecture review boards, cloud landing zones (Course 11), major programme design, mergers and divestitures.

## Learning objectives

1. Translate business goals, regulations and risks into security requirements.
2. Identify assets, trust zones and trust boundaries in a system.
3. Apply architecture principles (least privilege, defence in depth, secure defaults, fail safe, economy of mechanism, separation of duties).
4. Produce context, logical, data-flow, deployment and identity views.
5. Record architecture decisions with rationale and consequences.
6. Recognise legacy constraints and plan transitional architectures.
