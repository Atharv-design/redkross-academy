## Midnight Blizzard, 2023-24 - implicit trust in legacy test assets ---------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: Microsoft's guidance for responders (25 January 2024) states that Microsoft detected on 12 January 2024 an attack attributed to Midnight Blizzard. The actor used password spraying - with a low number of attempts to avoid detection - to compromise "a legacy, non-production test tenant account" that did not have MFA. It then used a legacy test OAuth application with elevated access to the Microsoft corporate environment to grant the Exchange Online full_access_as_app role, allowing access to mailboxes, and routed traffic through residential proxy networks to evade IP-based blocking. Microsoft's recommendations included auditing OAuth application permissions and using behaviour-based detection rather than relying on IP indicators [C22-M03-R4]. Zero trust lessons: - Tenet 1: test and legacy tenants, accounts and applications are resources and must be governed and verified like production. - Least privilege for applications (workload identities): standing tenant-wide mailbox access is excessive agency. - Tenet 4/7: dynamic, behaviour-informed policy beats static IP allow/deny lists when attackers use residential proxies. - Strong authentication everywhere - including non-production.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The trusted VPN subnet --------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. Tamarind's internal wiki allowed any connection from the VPN subnet without login, on the assumption that VPN users were trusted. A contractor's compromised laptop on the VPN browsed the wiki and found stored credentials. The fix: put the wiki behind the identity-aware proxy with SSO and MFA (removing location-based trust), purge secrets from the wiki (Course 12 M06), and add device posture requirements for contractors.
