## British Library, 2023 - legacy architecture and blast radius ----------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: The British Library's review "Learning lessons from the cyber-attack" (8 March 2024) describes the October 2023 ransomware attack claimed by Rhysida, which encrypted or destroyed much of the server estate and exfiltrated about 600GB of files. The review states that the Library's "unusually diverse and complex technology estate, including many legacy systems" contributed to the severity of the impact, and that the older network design "allowed the attackers wider access than would have been possible in a more modern network design". It also notes that reliance of older applications on manual processes increased the volume of staff and customer data held in multiple copies on the network [C22-M01-R1]. Architecture lessons: - Network design determines lateral reach; segmentation (Module 4) and identity-based access (Modules 2-3) limit it. - Data sprawl from legacy integration patterns enlarges what attackers can take; data architecture (Module 5) matters. - Legacy estates need transition architectures and funded retirement, not indefinite postponement.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The diagram from 2019 --------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. During an incident at Tamarind, responders used the only network diagram available - dated 2019. It omitted a partner VPN added in 2022 and a cloud database exposed to the office network for a migration. Containment decisions were delayed by hours. Afterwards the architecture team adopted diagrams-as-code generated partly from cloud inventory, dated views, and an ADR log, and added "diagrams current?" to the incident readiness checklist.
