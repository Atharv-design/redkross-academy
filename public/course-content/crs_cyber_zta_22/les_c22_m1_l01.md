**The security architect:**

- converts risk appetite and obligations into design requirements;
- designs patterns (reference architectures) teams can reuse - the "paved roads" of Course 12;
- reviews solution designs and approves deviations (exceptions, Course 17 Module 4);
- ensures controls compose correctly across layers (identity, network, application, data, operations);
- plans transitions from current to target state.

Frameworks such as SABSA (business-driven security architecture) and TOGAF (enterprise architecture) provide methods; NIST SP 800-207 and the CISA Zero Trust Maturity Model provide zero-trust structure (Module 3). The method matters less than consistency and traceability from requirement to control.
