WPA2 uses AES-CCMP for strong encryption and is still broadly acceptable, but its PERSONAL (pre-shared key) mode has a weakness: the four-way handshake can be captured and the passphrase subjected to an offline guessing attack, and a 2017 research result (the "KRACK" key-reinstallation attack) showed a flaw in the handshake that was subsequently patched. WPA3 addresses these: its personal mode uses SAE (Simultaneous Authentication of Equals), which resists offline dictionary attacks and provides forward secrecy, so capturing the handshake does not enable passphrase guessing [R1][R2]. WPA3 also mandates protected management frames. The defensive takeaway: prefer WPA3 for its stronger handshake; where WPA2 must be used, enforce a long, random passphrase (for personal mode) and keep firmware patched.
