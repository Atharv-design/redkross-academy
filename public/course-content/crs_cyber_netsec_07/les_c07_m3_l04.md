Every detector makes two kinds of error. A FALSE POSITIVE is a benign event flagged as malicious; a FALSE NEGATIVE is a malicious event missed. They trade off: making a detector more sensitive catches more real attacks (fewer false negatives) but flags more benign events (more false positives), and vice versa. The BASE-RATE problem makes this acute: malicious events are rare compared to benign traffic, so even a very accurate detector can produce mostly false positives in absolute numbers. This is why tuning and prioritisation matter more than raw sensitivity: an un-tuned sensor drowns the real alerts. The cost of each error differs by context - a false negative on a critical boundary is dangerous; a flood of false positives causes alert fatigue and missed real alerts.
