A network threat model asks, simply: what are we protecting, from whom, and where could they get in or move? You identify ASSETS (the database of customer data, the payment system, the management plane), ADVERSARIES and their goals (an external attacker seeking data; a compromised internal host seeking to move laterally), ENTRY POINTS (the internet-facing services, remote access, wireless, supply chain), and the PATHS an adversary could take from entry to asset. The output is a prioritised view of where boundaries and controls matter most. Threat modelling is done at design time and revisited as the network changes; it drives where you put firewalls, segmentation and monitoring. For the lab, the crown jewel is the customer database, and the key question is: from which zones can anything reach it, and should it?
