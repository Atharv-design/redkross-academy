At the mechanism level, networks are segmented by SUBNETS (IP ranges) and VLANs (virtual LANs that separate traffic at layer 2), with routing and firewalls controlling what passes between them. Putting the database tier in its own subnet/VLAN, separate from user workstations, means traffic between them must cross a routed boundary where a firewall or access control list can enforce policy. Without separation, hosts on the same segment can talk directly (layer 2), with no control point. Segmentation thus starts with physical or virtual separation and is enforced at the boundaries between segments. Modern virtualised and cloud networks add software-defined segmentation (security groups, network policies) that can isolate even individual workloads.
