A TLS server authenticates with a CERTIFICATE: a document binding the server's identity (domain) to a public key, signed by a Certificate Authority (CA) that the client trusts. The client verifies the certificate's signature chain up to a trusted root CA, checks the domain matches, and checks validity dates and revocation. This CHAIN OF TRUST is what lets a browser trust a server it has never met. Certificate hygiene matters: a strong key (RSA >= 2048 bits or an ECDSA key such as P-256), a valid and not-soon-expiring validity period, correct domain, and a trusted issuer. Expired certificates break connections (or train users to click through warnings); weak keys undermine the whole protection. Certificate lifecycle - issuance, renewal (ideally automated), and revocation - is a core operational task.
