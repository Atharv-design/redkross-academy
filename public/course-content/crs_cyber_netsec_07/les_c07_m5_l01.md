A Virtual Private Network (VPN) creates an encrypted tunnel across an untrusted network, so traffic inside it is confidential and integrity-protected and the endpoints are authenticated - effectively extending a trusted boundary. Two common patterns: REMOTE-ACCESS VPN (an individual user's device tunnels to the corporate network) and SITE-TO-SITE VPN (two networks, for example a branch and headquarters, are linked). Common technologies include IPsec (a suite operating at the network layer), TLS-based VPNs (operating over TLS), and WireGuard (a modern, simpler protocol). Design considerations: strong authentication (ideally certificate- or token-based with MFA), least-privilege access once connected (a VPN should not grant flat access to everything - combine it with segmentation and zero trust), and monitoring. A VPN protects the transit, but what the connected user may then reach must still be controlled.
