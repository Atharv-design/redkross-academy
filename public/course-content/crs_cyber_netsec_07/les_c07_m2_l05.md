EGRESS filtering controls OUTBOUND traffic from internal zones, and is often neglected because teams focus on keeping attackers out. But egress control is powerful: it limits what a compromised internal host can do - exfiltrate data, reach a command-and-control server, or pivot - by permitting only the specific outbound flows the business needs (for example, the app tier reaching an update server on 443, and nothing else). Default-deny applies outbound as well as inbound. The lab's pre-hardening policy has no egress rules at all (a finding); the hardened policy adds a single, specific egress allow (app->internet:443) and relies on default-deny for the rest, so a compromised host cannot freely call out.
