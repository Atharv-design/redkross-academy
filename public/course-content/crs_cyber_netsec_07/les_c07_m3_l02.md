SIGNATURE-based detection matches traffic against patterns of KNOWN bad activity - a specific byte sequence of an exploit, a known-malicious domain, a particular scan pattern. Its strengths: precise, low false positives for well-written signatures, and clear explanations ("matched rule X"). Its weakness: it only catches KNOWN threats - a novel attack with no signature slips through (a false negative), so signatures must be constantly updated. Most network IDS/IPS (such as Suricata and Snort, covered further in Course 20) are primarily signature engines with rule languages. Signatures are excellent for known exploits and malware but cannot, by design, catch the genuinely new.
