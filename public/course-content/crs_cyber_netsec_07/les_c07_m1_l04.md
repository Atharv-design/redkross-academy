Two principles from access control apply directly to networks. LEAST PRIVILEGE means each zone and host may reach only what it needs - the application tier reaches the database on one port, nothing more. DEFAULT-DENY means the policy denies everything not explicitly allowed: a firewall ends with "deny all", and a zero-trust segmentation matrix permits only listed flows. Together they invert the dangerous default of "allow unless blocked" into "block unless allowed", which is the single most important network-security posture. The lab's pre-hardening firewall lacks a default-deny (a HIGH finding) and contains an over-broad "users may reach anything" rule - both violations of these principles that the hardened policy fixes.
