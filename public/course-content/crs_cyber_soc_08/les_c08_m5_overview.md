## About this module

Threat intelligence is information about adversaries - their infrastructure, tools, behaviour and intent - that has been collected, analysed and put in context so that defenders can make better decisions. For a SOC analyst it appears mostly as indicators of compromise (IOCs) in feeds and as reports describing campaigns. This module teaches what kinds of indicators exist, how to judge their confidence and lifetime, how to use them to enrich and hunt, and - just as important - what intelligence cannot tell you.

## Why it matters

Used well, intelligence turns an ambiguous alert into a confirmed incident in minutes. Used badly, it floods the SOC with matches on stale addresses, leaks sensitive information shared under restrictions, or creates false confidence that "no IOC matches" means "no compromise".

## Where it is used

SIEM and EDR enrichment, blocklists, hunting, sector information-sharing communities (ISACs), CERT-In and other national advisories, executive briefings.

## Learning objectives

1. Classify IOC types and place them on the Pyramid of Pain.
2. Evaluate indicator confidence, source reliability and expiry.
3. Apply the Traffic Light Protocol (TLP 2.0) correctly.
4. Describe STIX 2.1 objects and how intelligence is exchanged.
5. Run an enrichment and matching workflow and interpret results.
6. Explain the limitations of intelligence and IOC-based detection.
