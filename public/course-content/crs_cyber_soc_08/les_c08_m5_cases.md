## FireEye red team tool countermeasures, 2020 ------------------------------------------------------------------ Context: when FireEye disclosed in December 2020 that its red team tools had been stolen (the intrusion described in Module 4), it published detection countermeasures publicly so other organisations could detect misuse of those tools. The GitHub repository "FireEye Red Team Tool Countermeasures" provides rules in Snort, YARA, ClamAV and HXIOC formats, labelled "Production" (expected to work with minimal tuning) or "Supplemental" (needing environment-specific tuning, often for hunting); it was archived read-only in May 2025 [C08-M05-R4]. Lessons: sharing detection content - not just hashes - helps many defenders; the Production/Supplemental labels are an explicit statement of confidence; community content still needs local testing and tuning; archived content should not be assumed current.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The leaked AMBER report ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. An analyst pasted IOCs from a TLP:AMBER ISAC report into a public social-media post asking "has anyone seen these?". The adversary, monitoring public channels, rotated infrastructure within hours, and the ISAC suspended the organisation's membership pending review. Lesson: TLP labels are operational security, not formality.
