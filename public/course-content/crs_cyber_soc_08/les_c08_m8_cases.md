## Volt Typhoon advisory, 2024 - hunting for living off the land ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: On 7 February 2024 CISA, NSA and FBI, with US and international partners (including the UK NCSC, Australia's ASD's ACSC, Canada's CCCS and New Zealand's NCSC-NZ), published advisory AA24-038A. It assessed with high confidence that PRC state-sponsored actors known as Volt Typhoon were pre-positioning on US critical infrastructure IT networks for possible disruptive attacks, described living-off-the-land techniques as a hallmark of their activity, and reported footholds maintained in some environments for at least five years [C08-M08-R1]. Hunting guidance in the advisory includes applying the companion joint guide on identifying living-off-the-land techniques; reviewing application, security and system logs, including Windows ESENT application log events 216, 325, 326 and 327 that may indicate copying of NTDS.dit; reviewing VPN and account logins for impossible travel and unusual frequency or duration; and checking directories such as C:\windows\temp\ and C:\users\public\ for unexpected files. Lessons: behaviour-based hypotheses (not just IOCs) are essential against LOTL; advisories are a direct source of hunt hypotheses; long dwell time means hunts must cover long retention periods - the CERT-In 180-day log requirement is a minimum, not a target.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The negative hunt ----------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. After an advisory, a SOC hunted for a listed web-shell path on all web servers and found nothing. The hunt record noted that two servers' logs were not forwarded. A follow-up after onboarding those logs found the web shell on one of them. The documented gap - not the negative result - led to the discovery.
