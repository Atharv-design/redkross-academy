## About this module

Detections find what someone anticipated; threat hunting looks for what they did not. A hunt starts from a hypothesis - "if an adversary were doing X here, we would see Y in our data" - and tests it with searches and analysis, producing either findings, new detections or a documented "not found with this data". This final module teaches hypothesis-driven hunting, then the documentation that makes SOC work usable by others: case records and the shift report. It closes by introducing the course capstone, which draws on every module.

## Why it matters

Some adversaries stay hidden for years. In its February 2024 advisory on the PRC state-sponsored group known as Volt Typhoon, CISA, NSA and FBI reported footholds maintained in some victim IT environments for at least five years, using "living off the land" techniques that blend in with administration [R1]. Alerts alone do not find that; deliberate hunting with good data can.

## Where it is used

Dedicated hunt teams, Tier 3 SOC analysts, MDR providers, post-incident scoping ("are they anywhere else?"), intelligence-driven sweeps after an advisory.

## Learning objectives

1. Formulate testable hunt hypotheses from intelligence, ATT&CK and environment knowledge.
2. Identify data requirements and check they are met before hunting.
3. Apply hunting techniques: searching, stacking, statistics, timeline and pivoting.
4. Record hunt results, including negative results, and turn findings into detections.
5. Write case documentation that another analyst can rely on.
6. Write a SOC shift report.

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** C08-M01 to C08-M07.

**Estimated study time:** About 8 hours (plus 12 hours capstone).
