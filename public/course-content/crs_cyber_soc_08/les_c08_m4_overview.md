## About this module

Triage is the decision, made many times a shift, about what an alert means and what happens next: close it, tune it, investigate further or escalate it as an incident. Good triage is fast without being careless. It rests on three skills taught here: prioritising by severity and context, enriching and validating alerts with evidence, and handling false positives in a way that improves detections rather than hiding them.

## Why it matters

Attackers rarely trigger one dramatic alert. The intrusion at Banyan Microfinance in this course's dataset produced seven rule hits across four log sources. A triage process that treats each one in isolation, or closes the "low" ones without a look, misses the story.

## Where it is used

Tier 1 and Tier 2 SOC work, managed detection and response providers, on-call security engineers in smaller organisations.

## Learning objectives

1. Assign severity and priority using impact, confidence and asset context.
2. Enrich alerts with asset, identity, network and history data.
3. Validate alerts by gathering corroborating or refuting evidence.
4. Classify outcomes (true positive, benign true positive, false positive).
5. Group related alerts into one case.
6. Handle false positives through documented tuning and suppression with expiry.
