## About this module

Every day, an organisation's systems generate millions of events. Somewhere among them may be the first signs of an attack: an odd process on a laptop, a burst of failed logins, a small regular connection to an unknown domain. A security operations centre exists to notice, understand and act on those signs quickly. This module explains what a SOC is, how it is organised into tiers and functions, how an alert moves from detection to closure, and how an analyst works through a shift. It introduces the course dataset - one day of activity at the fictional Banyan Microfinance - which you will investigate throughout the course.

## Why it matters

Detection tools are only as useful as the people and processes that act on their alerts. The 2013 Target breach analysis found automated warnings that were not acted upon effectively (case study). Organisations also face legal clocks: under India's CERT-In Directions, covered entities must report specified incidents within six hours of noticing them.

## Where it is used

In-house SOCs in banks, telecoms, government and large enterprises; managed security service providers (MSSPs) and managed detection and response (MDR) providers serving smaller organisations; hybrid models combining both.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain the mission and functions of a SOC.
2. Describe tiered and functional SOC models and the responsibilities of each role.
3. Trace the alert lifecycle from generation to closure.
4. Describe an analyst's shift workflow, handover and documentation.
5. Interpret SOC metrics and their pitfalls.
6. Compute basic SOC metrics from synthetic case data.
