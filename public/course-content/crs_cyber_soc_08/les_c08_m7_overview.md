## About this module

Triage ends when an analyst decides that an alert, or a group of alerts, is an incident. Then the work changes. More people are involved, decisions have business consequences, and legal and regulatory clocks may start running. This module covers the SOC analyst's part in incident handling: escalating clearly, communicating with the right people at the right time, recommending containment that is proportionate and safe, and preserving evidence so that investigators (Course 09), lawyers and regulators can rely on it.

## Why it matters

Organisations seldom fail incidents because nobody noticed. They fail because nobody escalated, the wrong people were told, containment destroyed evidence or tipped off the attacker, or a regulatory deadline was missed. In India, a reportable cyber incident must reach CERT-In within six hours of being noticed (Course 01 Module 7). That makes fast, accurate escalation a legal necessity.

## Where it is used

SOC escalation procedures, computer security incident response teams (CSIRTs), crisis management, legal and compliance reporting.

## Learning objectives

1. Place SOC incident handling in NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 and the CSF 2.0 framing.
2. Write a clear escalation using a standard structure.
3. Identify stakeholders and reporting obligations, including CERT-In and DPDP.
4. Recommend containment options and explain their trade-offs.
5. Preserve and document evidence with hashes and chain of custody.
6. Support recovery and post-incident lessons.

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** C08-M01 to C08-M06; Course 01 Module 7 (Indian legal framework).
