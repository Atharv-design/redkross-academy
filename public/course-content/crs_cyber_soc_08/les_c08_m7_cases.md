## Colonial Pipeline, 2021 - containment and early notification ----------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: In May 2021 Colonial Pipeline suffered a ransomware attack (Course 01 Module 2) and halted pipeline operations as a precaution, which led to fuel disruption in the eastern United States [C08-M07-R3]. On 7 June 2021 the US Department of Justice announced it had seized 63.7 bitcoins, then valued at about USD 2.3 million, traced from the ransom paid to the DarkSide group. Colonial had reported paying about 75 bitcoins. The announcement thanked Colonial for quickly notifying the FBI and said the result showed the value of early notification to law enforcement [C08-M07-R4]. Lessons: - Containment can have large business impact. Halting operations was a business decision taken to limit risk, and it should be planned before an incident. - Fast notification to the authorities enabled recovery of part of the payment. - Recording accurate times and facts supports both law enforcement and regulators. Verification note: the DOJ facts are cross-checked across multiple reports of the DOJ release (web search, 2026-11-07). The release page did not render for automated retrieval.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The helpful reboot ------------------------------------------------------------------------ This scenario is fictional. A help-desk technician, seeing a slow laptop flagged by the SOC, rebooted it and ran a "cleanup" tool before the SOC's escalation reached the incident lead. Memory evidence of the in-memory loader was lost, and the cleanup deleted the malicious document, removing the only copy. The lesson: escalation must reach operations teams with a clear "do not touch" instruction. It is part of containment.
