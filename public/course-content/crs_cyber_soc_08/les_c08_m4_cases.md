## FireEye, 2020 - following up a "routine" alert ---------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: In December 2020 FireEye (now Mandiant) disclosed that it had been breached, and its investigation led to the discovery of the SolarWinds Orion supply-chain compromise (Course 01 Module 6). In prepared testimony to the US Senate Select Committee on Intelligence (23 February 2021), FireEye's CEO Kevin Mandia described the initial detection: an alert showed that an employee's account had registered a second phone for multi-factor authentication. Such registrations are common, but the security team followed up, and the employee confirmed they had not registered a new device [C08-M04-R1] [C08-M04-R2]. Triage lessons: - A low-severity alert type, validated with one phone call to the user, revealed a nation-state intrusion. - Identity events such as MFA device changes are high-value telemetry. - Validation with the human involved is a legitimate and powerful step. ## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The muted rule -------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. A SOC received 300 alerts a day from a "new service installed" rule, mostly from software updates. An analyst added an exclusion for any service path containing "\Program Files\". Months later an attacker installed a malicious service under that path and no alert fired. A narrow exclusion by signed publisher, with a review date, would have kept coverage.
