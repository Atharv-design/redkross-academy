## About this module

Frameworks give organisations a common language for security outcomes and a structure for programmes. Standards add requirements that can be audited and certified. Regulations add obligations that must be met. Most organisations face several at once: a customer asks for ISO/IEC 27001 certification, a US partner speaks NIST CSF, an Indian regulator issues a framework, and the DPDP Act applies to personal data. This module explains NIST CSF 2.0 in detail, the concepts of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and its management-system approach, key Indian frameworks, and how to map one set of controls to many requirements without duplicating effort. The lab toolkit maps Tamarind's controls to CSF 2.0 and reports coverage gaps.

## Why it matters

Without mapping, compliance becomes many parallel projects, each with its own spreadsheets and audits. With a unified control set mapped to each framework, one control tested once can satisfy several requirements, and gaps become visible.

## Where it is used

Security programme design, certification projects, customer and regulator questionnaires, board reporting, and supplier requirements (Module 7).

## Learning objectives

1. Describe the structure of NIST CSF 2.0: Core, Profiles and Tiers.
2. Explain ISO/IEC 27001's management-system clauses, Annex A and the Statement of Applicability.
3. Identify relevant Indian frameworks and regulations and their scope.
4. Build a unified control set and map it to multiple frameworks.
5. Use Current and Target Profiles to plan improvement.
6. Recognise the limits of mapping and certification.
