## Capital One and the OCC, 2020 - risk assessment before change ------------------------------------------------------------------------ Context: On 6 August 2020 the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency assessed an USD 80 million civil money penalty against Capital One, N.A. and Capital One Bank (USA), N.A. The OCC cited the bank's "failure to establish effective risk assessment processes" before migrating significant information technology operations to the public cloud, and its failure to correct the deficiencies in a timely manner. The OCC described these as unsafe or unsound practices and violations of the Interagency Guidelines Establishing Information Security Standards. The OCC also issued a cease and desist order [C17-M02-R3]. The penalty followed the 2019 breach of customer data from the bank's cloud environment. Lessons: - Major changes such as a cloud migration, an acquisition or a new platform require risk assessment before and during the change, not after. - Regulators assess whether known deficiencies were remediated in time. Risk registers and treatment plans are evidence of that. - Risk management is a governance obligation with financial consequences, separate from the costs of the incident itself.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The optimistic control --------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. Tamarind rated R01 (ransomware) as residual "low" because "backups exist". An audit restore test found that backups were online and writable from the domain, so the same ransomware would encrypt them. The control effect was revised to near zero and residual risk rose to high. An immutable offline backup project was funded, and the register now requires control evidence before any reduction is claimed.
