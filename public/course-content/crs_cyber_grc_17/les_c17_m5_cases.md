## CrowdStrike content update outage, July 2024 -------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: On 19 July 2024 a faulty CrowdStrike Falcon content update caused Windows systems to crash. Microsoft estimated that about 8.5 million Windows devices were affected, less than one per cent of all Windows machines [C17-M05-R2]. CrowdStrike's root cause analysis describes a mismatch between expected and supplied input fields leading to an out-of-bounds read in kernel space (Course 12 Module 1). This was not a cyberattack, but it disrupted organisations worldwide, including airlines, healthcare and financial services, and many systems needed manual remediation. Resilience lessons: - Continuity plans must cover non-malicious, supplier-caused outages, not only attacks. - Manual workarounds, such as paper processes, and staff able to operate them, kept some services running. - Recovery at scale depends on access to recovery keys, such as disk-encryption recovery keys, and on hands-on capacity. These belong in DR prerequisites. - Supplier concentration risk (one agent on every endpoint) belongs in the BIA and in third-party risk (Module 7).



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The untested restore -------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. Tamarind's DR plan promised an 8-hour recovery of the shipment database. The first full restore test took 31 hours: the backup software licence for the DR site had lapsed, the restore documents referred to retired servers, and nobody had the decryption key password. The plan was rewritten from the test log, the key was escrowed, quarterly component tests were scheduled, and the achievable RTO in the BIA was corrected until improvements were proven.
