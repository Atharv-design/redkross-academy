| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Policy | high-level intent and principles. Approved by the board or executive committee, stable, and short. Example: "Customer data is protected according to its classification and access is limited to business need." |
| Standard | mandatory, specific requirements that support the policy. Approved by the CISO or a governance forum. Example: "MFA is required for all remote and administrative access." |
| Procedure | step-by-step how-to for a team. Owned by the operating team. Example: "How to onboard a user in the identity platform." |
| Guideline | recommended practice, not mandatory. Example: "Choosing a passphrase." |

Characteristics of a good hierarchy:

- every standard traces to a policy;
- every requirement is testable (Module 6) and has an owner;
- review dates are set (annual for policies, more frequent for fast-moving standards);
- an exception process exists (Module 4);
- publication is version-controlled;
- staff acknowledge relevant policies.

Policy hierarchies also map to frameworks. ISO/IEC 27001 requires an information security policy and documented information. CSF 2.0's GV.PO category concerns policy (Module 3).
