## Uber, 2016 breach - conviction for concealment (2022) ----------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: In October 2022 a US federal jury in San Francisco convicted Uber's former chief security officer, Joseph Sullivan, of obstruction of proceedings of the Federal Trade Commission and misprision of a felony, in connection with the concealment of a 2016 breach. Reporting of the case states that intruders accessed records of about 57 million users and 600,000 driver's licence numbers, and that the company paid the hackers USD 100,000 through its bug bounty programme while the FTC was already investigating Uber's security practices [C17-M01-R2]. The FTC's 2018 expanded settlement with Uber also states that the breach was not disclosed to consumers or the Commission until November 2017 (Course 12 Module 6). Governance lessons: - Disclosure and regulatory notification decisions belong to defined governance roles (legal, CEO, board), not to one individual. Clear escalation paths and records protect both the organisation and its staff. - "Bug bounty" or other programmes must not be used to avoid incident response and reporting obligations. - Security leaders need direct access to the board or audit committee for material incidents. - In India, comparable incidents would engage CERT-In's 6-hour reporting direction and DPDP obligations (Course 01 Module 7). Verification note: conviction details from the US Attorney's Office release as reported in multiple outlets (web-search cross-check); the sentencing and any appeal were not reviewed.
