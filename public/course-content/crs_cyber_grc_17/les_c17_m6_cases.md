## Equifax, 2017 - a detective control that silently failed ------------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: The US Government Accountability Office's report GAO-18-559 (August 2018) on the 2017 Equifax breach states that Equifax officials said the Apache Struts vulnerability (CVE-2017-5638) was not properly identified as present on the online dispute portal when patches were installed. The GAO also reports that a misconfiguration, caused by a digital certificate that had expired about 10 months earlier, allowed encrypted traffic to pass through without inspection. After the certificate was updated and inspection restarted, administrators recognised signs of intrusion, on 29 July 2017. At least 145.5 million individuals were affected [C17-M06-R1] (see Course 01 Module 1). Assurance lessons: - Test detective controls for operating effectiveness. "Is the monitoring actually seeing traffic?" is a simple test. A certificate expiry check or traffic-volume reconciliation would have found the gap. - Test completeness of inventories. The patch process depended on knowing where Struts ran, and a full-population software inventory test would have challenged that. - Auditors should ask for evidence of control output (alerts, inspected volumes), not only of configuration.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The attestation that was not evidence -------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. Each quarter, Tamarind's managers signed attestations that "access for my team has been reviewed". Internal audit re-performed the review for one quarter and found 19 inappropriate access rights, which the attesting managers had never actually reviewed. The review was moved into the identity platform with system-recorded decisions, and audit now tests the system records, not the signatures.
