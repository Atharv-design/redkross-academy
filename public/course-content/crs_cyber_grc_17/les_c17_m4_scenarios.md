## Scenario 1

A team requests a permanent exception to the patching standard for a production OT controller.

**Task:** Respond.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> There are no permanent exceptions. Grant a time-limited exception with compensating controls (network segmentation, allowlisted access, monitoring, vendor-approved patch windows) and a risk register entry, approved at the right level. Plan vendor engagement and replacement, and review at each expiry. Consider an OT-specific standard if the general one cannot fit.
>
> **Lesson:** exceptions are temporary and compensated.

## Scenario 2

Legal says the incident policy does not reflect CERT-In's 6-hour requirement.

**Task:** Update the documents.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Update the incident policy statement (notification obligations), the incident standard (classification of reportable incidents, a 6-hour target, points of contact) and the procedure (who reports, how, and the records kept). Add the DPDP intimation steps once applicable. Train the response team, test in a tabletop exercise, and record the change.
>
> **Lesson:** legal obligations must flow through all document layers.

## Scenario 3

Auditors find standards that contradict each other on password length.

**Task:** Fix.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Create a single authentication standard aligned with NIST SP 800-63B-4 (Course 01): length rules and breached-password screening, no forced periodic changes. Retire the conflicting documents, map the remaining ones, communicate, and update technical settings to match.
>
> **Lesson:** one requirement, one source.
