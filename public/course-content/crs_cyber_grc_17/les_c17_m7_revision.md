## Revision

### Summary

Third parties create data, access, software, service and fourth-party exposure, and DPDP keeps the Data Fiduciary responsible for its processors. Inventory vendors and tier them by inherent risk. Due diligence scales from questionnaires and evidence review (certificate scope) to assessments and audits. Critical items must override averages. In the tested toolkit, QuickPrint was rejected for MFA and notification failures despite a total score, while RouteOpt was approved with a regional condition. Contracts set the security baseline, DPDP processor duties, notification timeframes that support CERT-In's 6 hours, audit rights, SLAs aligned with the BIA, and exit terms. Monitoring is periodic and trigger-based, and vendor incidents follow a defined playbook. Manage concentration risk and plan exits. MOVEit (AA23-158A) showed how one product can expose many organisations directly and through their vendors.

### Important concepts

tiering; critical-fail scoring; contracts as controls.

### Key technical takeaways

questionnaire design; evidence verification; inventory queries.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concentration risk | Excessive dependence on one provider. |
| Fourth party | A vendor's vendor or sub-processor. |
| Sub-processor | Party processing data on a processor's behalf. |
| Tiering | Classifying vendors by inherent risk. |
| TPRM | Third-party risk management. |

### Common mistakes

one-size questionnaires; trusting answers without evidence; no exit plans.

### Revision questions

​1. Define three vendor tiers. 2. List seven contract clauses. 3. How do you respond to a vendor product advisory?
