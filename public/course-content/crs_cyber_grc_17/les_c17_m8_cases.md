## Cyber Safety Review Board, 2024 - governance lessons from Storm-0558 -------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: On 2 April 2024 the US Department of Homeland Security announced the Cyber Safety Review Board's report on the summer 2023 Microsoft Exchange Online intrusion attributed to Storm-0558. Its findings, as summarised in the announcement and coverage, include that the intrusion was preventable and resulted from a cascade of security failures, and that the actor used a 2016 consumer signing key that remained valid in 2023. The method of key theft had not been determined. About 22 organisations and more than 500 individuals were affected. The Board made recommendations for cloud service providers and the wider ecosystem [C17-M08-R1] (see Courses 01 and 15). Governance lessons for a management review: - Major incidents call for an independent, structured review whose recommendations reach top management and the board. - Key and identity infrastructure risks belong on the risk register at executive level, with lifecycle controls tested (Module 6). - Track recommendations to completion and report progress. A review without follow-through repeats the risk. - Transparency with customers and regulators about findings and actions is part of governance.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The review that only "noted" -------------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. For two years, Tamarind's management review minutes recorded "risk register noted; audit report noted". No acceptance or funding decisions were taken. When the leaver finding recurred, the auditor rated management review itself ineffective. The new format circulated decision papers a week ahead, required each high risk to end in a decision (fund, accept with expiry, or escalate to the board), and tracked actions publicly at the next review.
