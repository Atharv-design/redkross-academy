## British Library, 2023 - the MFA scope decision ---------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: The British Library's review "Learning lessons from the cyber-attack" (8 March 2024) describes the ransomware attack claimed by the Rhysida group and identified as a major incident on 28 October 2023. The first detected unauthorised access was at a Terminal Services server installed in 2020 for trusted external partners and internal IT administrators. That server was protected by firewalls and anti-virus but was not subject to MFA. When MFA was introduced in 2020 for cloud applications, "for reasons of practicality, cost and impact on ongoing Library programmes", connectivity to the Library's domain was placed out of scope pending infrastructure renewal. The review states that the lack of MFA on the domain was identified and raised as a risk at the time, "but the possible consequences were perhaps under-appraised". About 600GB of files, including personal data of users and staff, were exfiltrated. One of its lessons is to implement MFA on all internet-facing endpoints, "regardless of any technical difficulties in doing so" [C17-M04-R1]. Lessons: - A scoping decision is an exception, even if it is not labelled as one. It needs risk appraisal, compensating controls, an owner and an expiry. - "Pending further renewal" without a date is effectively permanent. - Exceptions on remote-access paths deserve escalation to senior governance and short review cycles. - The organisation documented the risk, which is better than many. But documentation without timely treatment did not prevent the impact.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The policy nobody could follow ----------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. Tamarind's 2019 standard required "encryption of all removable media" but provided no tool. Staff copied files to USB drives anyway. An audit found 80% non-compliance and no exceptions filed. Rather than disciplining staff, the committee funded a managed encrypted-drive solution, updated the procedure, and set a 90-day transition with exceptions for specific devices. Compliance was measured automatically through endpoint controls.
