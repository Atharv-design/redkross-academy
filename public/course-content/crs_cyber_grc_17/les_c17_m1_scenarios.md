## Scenario 1

The CISO reports to the CIO, who wants to delay a critical finding's escalation.

**Task:** Advise on governance.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Use the committee charter's escalation route. The CISO should have direct access to the risk or audit committee for material issues. Document the finding, the risk rating and the CIO's position, and present options. If the structure prevents escalation, recommend that the board review the CISO's reporting line or add a dotted line to the CEO or CRO.
>
> **Lesson:** independence and escalation paths are design choices.

## Scenario 2

A new board asks, "Are we secure?"

**Task:** Frame the answer.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> **Reframe it:** "secure enough for our risk appetite?" Present the top risks with trend and treatment, key control indicators against targets, recent incidents and lessons, assurance results, and the decisions or investment needed. Agree a risk appetite statement if none exists.
>
> **Lesson:** security is managed risk, not a yes/no.

## Scenario 3

Policies are 40 pages each and nobody reads them.

**Task:** Fix.

> [!TIP] Model answer
> Split them into short policies (intent, 1-2 pages) and separate standards and procedures. Write testable statements, assign owners and review dates, publish searchable versions, require acknowledgement, and train on the key points.
>
> **Lesson:** usable documents get followed.
