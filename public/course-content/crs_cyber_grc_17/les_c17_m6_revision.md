## Revision

### Summary

Assurance tests whether controls work, through self-assessment, second-line review, internal audit, external audit and certification, regulatory inspection and technical testing. Audits are planned with objective, scope, criteria, risk focus and approach. Evidence must be sufficient, reliable, relevant and timely. Direct and system evidence outranks attestations and inquiry. Controls are tested for design and operating effectiveness by inspection, observation, re-performance and analytics. Sampling needs recorded methods and tolerable rates. In the tested lab, a seeded 25-item sample found no exceptions, while full reconciliation found four leaver accounts still active. Findings state condition, criteria, cause, effect, recommendation, rating and management response, and closure needs a re-test. The Equifax certificate expiry (GAO) shows why detective controls need operating-effectiveness testing.

### Important concepts

evidence reliability; sampling risk; validated closure.

### Key technical takeaways

seeded sampling; full-population joins; finding structure.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Design effectiveness | Whether a control as designed meets its objective. |
| Operating effectiveness | Whether it worked consistently in the period. |
| Re-performance | Auditor repeats the control. |
| Sampling risk | Risk that a sample misrepresents the population. |
| Tolerable rate | Maximum acceptable exception rate. |

### Common mistakes

relying on attestations; over-claiming from samples; closing without re-test.
