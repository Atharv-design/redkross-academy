A security steering or risk committee charter, kept to one page, contains:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Purpose | oversee information security and cyber risk on behalf of the executive committee and board. |
| Membership | CEO (chair) or delegate, CISO (secretary), CIO, CRO, CFO, legal, DPO and business unit heads. Internal audit attends as an observer. |
| Authority | approve standards; accept residual risks up to a defined level (for example residual score 12 on the 5x5 scale), with anything above going to the board risk committee; approve the security budget proposal; commission reviews. |
| Inputs | risk register (Module 2), KRIs and KPIs, incident reports, audit findings (Module 6), vendor risk summary (Module 7), exception register (Module 4). |
| Outputs | minutes recording decisions and owners; a board report each quarter. |
| Cadence | monthly, plus ad hoc for major incidents. |
| Review | the charter is reviewed annually. |

**Two design points:**

- Decision thresholds make accountability explicit. Without them, every risk is "noted" and nobody accepts it.
- Minutes are evidence. Auditors and regulators will ask who decided, when, and on what information.
