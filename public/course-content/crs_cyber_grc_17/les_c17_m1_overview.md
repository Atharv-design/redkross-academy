## About this module

Security governance is the system by which an organisation directs and controls security. It sets objectives, assigns accountability, decides how much risk is acceptable, allocates resources, and checks that what was decided is actually happening. Management runs security day to day. Governance makes sure the right things are being run, by the right people, with honest reporting upwards. This module explains governance structures and roles from the board to the system owner, how accountability differs from responsibility, and how a policy hierarchy turns intent into enforceable rules.

## Why it matters

When governance is weak, technical teams make risk decisions that belong to executives, problems are hidden instead of escalated, and nobody can show regulators who decided what. The 2022 conviction of Uber's former chief security officer for concealing a breach from the US Federal Trade Commission shows the personal and organisational stakes (case study).

## Where it is used

Boards and risk committees, CISO offices, regulated entities in banking, securities, insurance, telecoms and government, and any company subject to customer security reviews or certification.

## Learning objectives

1. Define governance, management and operations and how they interact.
2. Describe the roles of the board, executives, CISO, risk, audit and owners.
3. Apply the three lines model and distinguish accountability from responsibility.
4. Build a RACI for security processes.
5. Design a policy hierarchy and explain how each layer is approved and maintained.
6. Report security to the board in decision-useful form.
