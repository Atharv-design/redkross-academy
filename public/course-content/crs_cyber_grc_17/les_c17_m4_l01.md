A policy expresses management intent and principles. A typical structure, in 1 to 3 pages:

1. Purpose: why the policy exists, linked to business objectives and risks.
2. Scope: people, systems, data, locations and third parties covered.
3. Policy statements: short principles in "must" language.
4. Roles and responsibilities: owners, approvers and everyone's duties.
5. Compliance: monitoring, the consequences of breaches and the exception route.
6. Related documents: the standards and procedures that implement it.
7. Document control: version, owner, approver, approval date, next review.

**Style:**

- Plain English, with "must" for mandatory and "should" for recommended. Define terms once.
- No technology brand names. Those belong in standards.
- Consistency with law and regulation. For example, a data protection policy reflects DPDP obligations, and an incident policy reflects the CERT-In 6-hour reporting requirement and internal escalation.

**Example statement:** "Access to information systems must be granted on a need-to-know basis, approved by the asset owner, and reviewed at least quarterly for critical systems."
