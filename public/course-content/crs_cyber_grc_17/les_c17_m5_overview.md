## About this module

Resilience is the ability to keep delivering critical services through disruption and to recover quickly afterwards. Disruptions include ransomware, cloud or vendor outages, faulty updates, power and site loss, and pandemics. Business continuity management starts with a business impact analysis (BIA), which determines what matters most and how quickly it must return. Continuity plans keep processes running, often with manual workarounds. Disaster recovery (DR) restores the technology. This module covers BIA, recovery objectives, continuity and DR strategies, testing, and governance. The lab toolkit checks whether system recovery capabilities actually support the business's recovery objectives.

## Why it matters

On 19 July 2024 a faulty software update caused about 8.5 million Windows devices to crash worldwide (case study). Airlines, hospitals and banks found out within hours whether their continuity plans worked. Ransomware recovery often takes months; the British Library was still restoring services in 2024 after its October 2023 attack (Module 4).

## Where it is used

Business continuity and DR programmes; regulatory requirements for financial and critical sectors (for example RBI expectations on business continuity, included in its IT governance Master Direction); customer contracts with availability commitments; insurance.

## Learning objectives

1. Conduct a BIA: processes, impacts over time, MTD, RTO and RPO, dependencies.
2. Verify that technology recovery capabilities meet business objectives.
3. Select continuity and DR strategies.
4. Write and maintain continuity and DR plans, including cyber scenarios.
5. Test plans through tabletop, walkthrough and technical recovery tests.
6. Govern resilience with metrics and reporting.
