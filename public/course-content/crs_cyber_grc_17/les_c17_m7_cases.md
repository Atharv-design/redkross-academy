## MOVEit Transfer, 2023 - one product, many victims ------------------------------------------------------------------------- Context: CISA and FBI advisory AA23-158A (7 June 2023) reported that the CL0P ransomware gang (also known as TA505) exploited CVE-2023-34362, an SQL injection vulnerability in Progress Software's MOVEit Transfer, beginning on 27 May 2023 according to the open-source information cited, and installed a web shell named LEMURLOOT to steal data [C17-M07-R1] (see Course 12 Module 3). Organisations were exposed both by running MOVEit themselves and through service providers that used it to handle their data. Third-party risk lessons: - Inventory must include products used by your vendors to process your data (fourth-party exposure), not just your direct vendors. - Contracts must require prompt notification of vendor-side incidents and cooperation in investigations. - When an advisory names a widely used product, TPRM needs a fast way to ask "which vendors and systems use this?", with prepared questionnaires and contacts. - File-transfer and integration platforms concentrate sensitive data, so tier them as critical.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The label printer vendor ----------------------------------------------------------------------- This scenario is fictional. A Tamarind depot manager onboarded QuickPrint Labels without procurement review. Its remote support tool had no MFA (the lab's V2 critical fail) and connected to the depot network. An assessment after the fact led to suspended remote access, installation of an approved remote-access gateway with MFA and session recording, contract clauses for 24-hour breach notification, and a rule that any vendor with network access is Tier 1 regardless of contract value.
