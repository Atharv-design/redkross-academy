## CA/Browser Forum SC-081v3, 2025 - 47-day certificates ---------------------------------------------------------------- Context: On 11 April 2025, the CA/Browser Forum's Server Certificate Working Group passed ballot SC-081v3, "Introduce Schedule of Reducing Validity and Data Reuse Periods". It reduces maximum public TLS certificate validity from 398 days to 47 days in stages between March 2026 and March 2029, with data-reuse periods for domain validation also reduced [C15-M06-R3]. As of this course's research date (November 2026), the first step (200 days) is already in force according to the published schedule. Lessons: - Certificate operations must be automated. Organisations renewing by hand once a year will face renewal every few weeks. - Shorter lifetimes reduce the window in which a compromised or mis-issued certificate is useful, and reduce dependence on revocation. - Inventory is a prerequisite: you cannot automate renewal for certificates you do not know about. Appliances and partner-managed endpoints are typical gaps. - Private PKIs are not bound by this ballot, but the same benefits apply. Many organisations adopt short lifetimes internally.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The forgotten load balancer ------------------------------------------------------------------------ This scenario is fictional. Tamarind automated renewal for its web servers, but the public certificate on an old load balancer was installed manually. It expired on a Saturday, and the courier partner API failed for six hours. The review added the load balancer to ACME automation and created a certificate inventory from CT logs and network scans of Tamarind's own ranges. Expiry alerts now go to an on-call rota at 30, 14 and 7 days.
