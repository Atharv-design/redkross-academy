## Zoom meetings, 2020 - AES-128 in ECB mode ----------------------------------------------------------------- Context: On 3 April 2020, Citizen Lab researchers Bill Marczak and John Scott-Railton reported that Zoom, whose documentation described AES-256 encryption, used a single AES-128 key per meeting in ECB mode, shared by all participants to encrypt and decrypt audio and video. They noted that ECB is not recommended because patterns in the plaintext are preserved. The meeting keys were generated and distributed by Zoom servers. In one test with participants in the United States and Canada, a key was delivered from a server that appeared to be in Beijing. Zoom had published a blog post on 1 April 2020 clarifying that it did not implement end-to-end encryption as most people understand the term [C15-M02-R2]. Lessons: - Using a strong cipher (AES) with the wrong mode (ECB) undermines confidentiality, as the lab shows. - Key management and distribution, meaning who generates keys and where they travel, is as important as the algorithm. - Security claims in documentation must match the implementation. Review and independent testing find gaps.



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): The swapped salary ------------------------------------------------------------------------ This scenario is fictional. Tamarind encrypted employee salary fields with AES-GCM but without associated data. A database administrator copied one employee's encrypted salary field into another employee's row. It decrypted successfully, because the ciphertext was valid. Adding the employee ID and field name as AAD made such swaps fail with InvalidTag. Integrity monitoring now alerts on decryption failures.
