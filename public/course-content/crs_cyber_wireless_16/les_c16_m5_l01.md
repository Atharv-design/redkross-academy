Android isolates each app in a SANDBOX. Historically each app runs as its own Linux user ID, so the operating system's user-based permissions keep one app's process and files separate from another's; apps cannot read each other's private data or interfere with each other's processes by default. This sandbox is the foundation of mobile security: it means a malicious or buggy app is contained, and an app's private data is private FROM OTHER APPS by default. The sandbox is enforced by the kernel and the platform, not by the app, so it holds even if the app is poorly written. The defender's concern is therefore less "is there a sandbox" (there is) and more "does the app keep its sensitive data INSIDE the sandbox and avoid handing it out" - which depends on storage choices (L03) and component exposure (L04) [R1].
