Protected Management Frames (PMF, IEEE 802.11w) cryptographically protect certain management frames - crucially the DEAUTHENTICATION and disassociation frames - so they cannot be forged. Before PMF, an attacker could forge a deauth to knock clients off a network (a denial of service) or to push them toward an evil twin; PMF stops this. The Wi-Fi Alliance states that PMF is REQUIRED for all new certified devices and that WPA3 networks must require PMF; it extends protection to unicast and multicast management action frames [R1]. For a defender, "is PMF required on this WLAN?" is a top-priority configuration question - its absence is a high-severity finding. The lab's wifireview.py flags PMF disabled as HIGH.
