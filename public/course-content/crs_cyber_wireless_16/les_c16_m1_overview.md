## About this module

Wireless security starts with understanding the medium. This module covers the radio and WLAN concepts behind Wi-Fi, the architecture of access points and clients, and the threat surface that a shared, invisible medium creates. Because anyone within range can hear the radio, wireless defence is different from wired defence - and this module builds the mental model the rest of the course relies on, framed strictly for the defender who reviews and hardens their own networks.

## Why it matters

A wireless network broadcasts into space that you do not control: the car park, the next floor, the street. That changes the threat model fundamentally - an attacker needs no cable and no port, only proximity. Understanding how Wi-Fi works (channels, SSIDs, association, frames) tells you what an attacker can see and do over the air, and therefore what you must protect: the confidentiality of traffic, the integrity of management frames, and the authenticity of the access point itself. Without these foundations, Wi-Fi hardening is a checklist; with them, it is reasoned defence. This module is the groundwork for every later wireless module.

## Where it is used

Wireless network design and security; site surveys and WLAN deployment; SOC monitoring of wireless threats; and the architecture decisions (segmentation, guest networks, PMF) that follow from the threat model. It is the shared vocabulary for securing any Wi-Fi estate.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain radio and WLAN basics: bands, channels, SSIDs and association.
2. Describe access-point and client architecture and frame types.
3. Explain why the shared medium changes the threat model.
4. Describe the Wi-Fi threat surface (eavesdropping, rogue/evil-twin, deauth).
5. Explain the defender's goals: confidentiality, integrity, authenticity.
6. Frame wireless assessment as authorised and offline in this course.

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** Basic networking (Course 02): TCP/IP, LANs, the idea of a layer-2 network.

**Estimated study time:** About 8 hours.
