## ingress-nginx admission controller, CVE-2025-1974 Context: Kubernetes issue 131009, opened on 23 March 2025, disclosed CVE-2025-1974 in the ingress-nginx controller, scored CVSS 3.1 9.8 [C21-M08-R1]. What happened: the advisory states that under certain conditions an unauthenticated attacker with access to the pod network could achieve arbitrary code execution in the context of the ingress-nginx controller, which could lead to disclosure of Secrets the controller can access - by default, Secrets cluster-wide. Affected were versions before v1.11.0, v1.11.0 to 1.11.4 and v1.12.0; fixes were v1.11.5 and v1.12.1. Before patching, the issue could be mitigated by disabling the controller's validating admission functionality. The advisory reported no known indicators of compromise. Lessons for the capstone: - Inventory add-ons: the first question was "do we run ingress-nginx, and which version?" - an inventory and SBOM question (L03). - Network exposure inside the cluster matters: "access to the pod network" is exactly what default-deny NetworkPolicy and controlled ingress to admission webhooks limit (Module 5). - RBAC of add-ons is blast radius: a controller that can read every Secret turns one code-execution bug into cluster-wide credential exposure (Module 4). Review add-on permissions in the RBAC register. - Admission webhooks are critical infrastructure (Module 4 L06).



## (FICTIONAL TEACHING SCENARIO): Audit week Context: Kestrel Pay (fictional) faced its first external audit of the payment platform. Hardening had been done a year earlier by a consultant; the evidence was a set of screenshots. What happened: auditors asked for current evidence. Reproducing the screenshots took two weeks and showed that three controls had silently regressed: a new namespace without Pod Security labels, an add-on with a cluster-wide secrets grant, and audit logging lost during an upgrade. Response: the team built an evidence collector like evidence.py, ran it daily with results stored in write-once storage and alerts on any FAIL, and added it to the upgrade checklist. The next audit took two days. Lessons: evidence is a product with an owner. Automated, repeatable evidence turns audits from archaeology into review and detects regressions between audits.
